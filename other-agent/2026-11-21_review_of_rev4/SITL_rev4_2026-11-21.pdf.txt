                               BinRobo revision 4 - ArduPilot SITL re-test (driving software)
         Independent re-run of the frozen B2-F 60-run suite at 177.6 kg with their revision 4 companion, 21 Nov 2026




Verdict
Revision 4 scores 47 / 60 on the frozen SITL criteria (ArduPilot Rover 4.6.2, frozen 50 Wh scorer). They report 48 /
60. The extra failure is a fault run (s4c seed 11) that ended on a different alarm from the expected one. The robot was safe and
stopped.

trip limit (C6 only changed)    50 Wh (frozen)        65 Wh                         70 Wh                         75 Wh
runs passing, of 60             47                    47                            50                            52



Raising the trip limit no longer gets to 60 / 60. Rev 3 reached 60 / 60 at 65 Wh. In rev 4, 8 runs fail for reasons that have
nothing to do with energy:
   • 6 x “too late”: all six three-bin evenings (s3a and s3b, every seed). Each ran two trips, then refused the third because the
     companion’s own 18:00-21:00 window said it would end after 21:00. The cause is the slower speed rule: trips now take 64 to 74
     min.
   • 1 x hedge clearance (s1b seed 23): 0.098 m against the 0.124 m limit (C1). This happened at pad A while the robot
     was re-aligning on the bin after the camera found it 204 mm off. This is new: the worst in rev 3 was 0.184 m, and the worst in
     their rev 4 run was 0.198 m.
   • 1 x wrong alarm (s4c seed 11): during the long RTK loss the robot stopped (HOLD) at station 86 m as it should. While it
     stood still, the EKF estimate drifted 14 m, and the firmware fence fired before the companion’s 60 s no-fix alarm. The robot
     was safe and did not move, but the outcome label is wrong for the scorer. This is a race between two alarms.
The safety logic they added works. The camera-only gate, the 3-frame average, the seating check and the closed refusals all
behave as described. The seating check is now the thing that makes a camera-only gate safe: with a 60 mm camera bias
the gate passed robots that were truly 39 to 77 mm off, and the seating check caught every one. With the seating check off, the
robot lifted bins 69 mm off centre (2 of 2). That is outside the 50 mm pick-up envelope.
The 18:00-21:00 window is our own B2-F companion rule, not the bye-law. The bye-law is: bins out no earlier than 18:00
the day before, and back by 21:00 on collection day. Under the real bye-law:
   • three drops from 18:00 fit: they end about 21:25-21:35 the evening before, and nothing in the bye-law limits that.
   • three returns from 18:00 on collection day do not fit: they end about 21:35-21:40. They fit if they start by about 17:20 (17:00
     with a 10 % margin), any time after the collector has been.

5. Timing against the [county] bye-law
The bye-law: out no earlier than 18:00 the day before collection; back by 21:00 on collection day. The companion (our
own B2-F code, frozen C3) applies a stricter 18:00-21:00 window on both evenings and assumes 50 min per trip.

D-1, drops (s3a)                  first motion >= 18:00; no end time       18:00 + 73.5 + 64.9 = 20:19, third     yes (bye-law); no under our 21:00
                                                                           drop about 65-74 min -> back           window
                                                                           about 21:25-21:33
D0, returns (s3b)                 back by 21:00; start may be any          from 18:00: 73.1 + 72.3 + about 72     no from 18:00; yes if started by
                                  time after the collection                -> about 21:37                         about 17:20 (17:00 with a 10 %
                                                                                                                  margin)



So the three drop-evening “too late” refusals (s3a) are caused by our own window, not the bye-law. The three collection-day refusals
(s3b) are real unless the returns start earlier. Recommended (requirements for Brian):
   1. D-1: start at 18:00 and drop the 21:00 cut-off. Keep a quiet-hours limit if Brian wants one (for example 22:00).
   2. D0: start returns once the collector has been (or from a set time such as 16:00), with 21:00 as the deadline.
   3. Replace the hard-coded 50 min with the measured trip time for that bin and pad, plus a margin, in the start check. As coded,
      a trip started between 19:46 and 20:10 on collection day would breach 21:00.
   4. Their three alternatives (faster rule, castor brakes) bring trips to 54-64 min. That fits from 18:00, but the faster rule breaks
      their own stopping-distance limit (146-167 mm against the 150 mm assumed). Starting earlier costs no hardware.

8. Not run / limits
       • Camera/lidar stress: seed 7 only, one-bin missions only. Not run: camA s1b, cam15, lid15 s1b, night (camA + lid15), camera
         stress on s3a/s3b, m195 on s1b / s3b and other seeds.
       • 4.7.x not run (4.6.2 only). Differences against their runs (s4c s11 alarm race, s1b s23 bin estimate, their 2 refusals) are firmware-
         and seed-dependent and were not investigated further.
       • Their alternative speed rules (faster rule, castor brakes) were not re-run.
       • No run of a third trip past 21:00 or an earlier D0 start: the timing in section 5 is worked out from the measured trip times, not
         simulated.
       • The seating sensor and camera yaw are idealised in their code (no bias), and the final reverse still steers on truth plus a constant
         offset. Dock offsets (max 18.9 mm) are therefore optimistic.
