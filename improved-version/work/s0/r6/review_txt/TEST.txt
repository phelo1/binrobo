1. Summary
Verdict: revision 5.1 reproduces on the lane as modelled (EU-DEM profile, steepest 8.4 %), but it has
almost no margin, and the margin is gone if the hill has any short section steeper than the DEM shows,
or if the robot meets the slope at level speed.
  • Reproduces (stand-in hardware, see 2): 15 of their 38 stop points re-run. 12 are within 7 mm of theirs
    (level empty 0.4 m/s 139/139 mm, level full 119/118, 3 % full 0.4 143/142, 3 % empty 0.4 178/176, 8.5 % empty
    0.2 221/223, 9 % empty 0.15 174/172, 10 % empty grip 0.30 92/93, and others). 10 % empty on wet leaves slides
    here too (1.47 m against their 1.41 m).
  • Does not reproduce: their headline point, 8.5 % empty on wet leaves at 0.15 m/s: 170 mm here
    against their 120 mm. It sits on a cliff edge: 8.0 % 105 mm, 8.25 % 146, 8.5 % 170, 9 % 174, 9.5 % 307, 10 %
    1.47 m, 11 % 8.1 m, 12 % 13.3 m (runaway). Two smaller differences: 10 % full bin 0.2 m/s 156/139 mm, 8.5 %
    empty grip 0.30 80/64 mm. All three involve the drive wheels close to their grip limit, where the missing rev5
    mass table (CG position) matters most.
  • Whole lane on the real slope at grip 0.22 with the 5.1 rule: P1, P2a, P4a, P4b, P6a, X8n and X9n all
    pass. They match their table closely: X8n gap 1.75 m (theirs 1.74), P6a 1.66 m (1.66), P4a/P4b creep 1.6 mm
    (1.6), X9n creep 0.5 mm (their 5.1 run 2.5 mm), P2a 33.6 Wh (33.6).
  • New finding 1, short ramp: we made the steepest 25 m of the hill (133-158 m) steeper than the DEM shows.
    The empty robot on wet leaves:
       – at 9 % it holds;
       – at 10 % the stop-and-hold slides 409 mm in 5 s (FAIL);
       – at 11 % the person test stops 28 mm from the person (e-stop 0.85 m), and the hold run cannot stop on the
         ramp;
       – at 12 % it runs away to 1.9-2.7 m/s, hits the person in X8n and hits the hedge in both runs.
    On a wet surface without leaves (grip 0.30), 12 % still passes.
    With a full bin it holds even at 12 % (creep 4 mm). Short pitches of this size do not show up in a 25 m DEM, so
    the slope must be measured on site before the 8.4 % figure is relied on.
  • New finding 2, meeting the slope at speed: a touch while the slope changes from level to 8.5 %, with the
    robot still at the level speed of 0.4 m/s, gives 496 mm (empty, wet leaves) and 227 mm (empty, wet). A full bin
    gives 215 mm. In the lane runs, a 3 m look-ahead on the mapped slope keeps the robot slow enough; on the real
    robot that only works if the map is right.
  • Probes:
       – reaction 0.20 s: 8.5 % empty 178 mm, level empty 159 mm;
       – +10 % mass (198 kg): 8.5 % empty 143 mm, 9 % empty 204 mm;
       – grip 0.18: 8.5 % empty slides 1.17 m, full 125 mm, level empty 165 mm.
  • Carried open, unchanged: the 40 mm sharp step, the open 60 mm pothole and the open trench all still stall
    (s 291.0 / 550.5 / 454.2, the same as rev4 and their files).

2. The stand-in, and how close it is
The revision 5 hardware pack (BinRobo_rev5_tests_2026-11-21.zip, with mass_cgB2_r5.json and the rev5
simB2hx) was not sent to us. We used our rev4 setup with their rev5 settings:
  • the revision 4 simulator (simB2hx_r4);
  • their rev5 settings (X_NOSE_DX 0.245, rocker, lift 6800 N, the same CGB_GEO);
  • the rev4 mass table scaled to 180 kg (h2/mass_cgB2_r4_m180.json: rows and carriage x1.01399).
How close the stand-in is:
  • JSON header check: we compared the header of one of their files (8.5 % empty, wet leaves) with ours at the
    same settings. Only CGB_MASSF and CMP_SIM differ. All 40 geo entries are identical, and the reported robot mass
    is the same (172.5 kg, the pstop figure that leaves out part of the carriage).
  • Driver script: their runY.py differs from ours only in the lowering test (ToF sensor, X_TRIP_N). The person-stop
    code is identical.
  • X_NOSE_DX (0.245 against 0.34) makes no difference to pstop: both give 170 mm.
The stand-in therefore has the right total mass and geometry but not their CG. Where braking is well inside the grip
limit, it matches to within a few mm. Near the limit (empty robot on wet leaves, 8.5 % and up) the CG decides how
much weight is on the braked drive wheels, and there we differ by up to 50 mm. The rev5 mass table is needed to
settle the 8.5 % point.

Runner caveat: their lanerun_x.py X9n verdict checks only the creep where the robot ended up holding. In s11 the
robot could not stop at 146 m, slid to 161 m, then “held” on the 4.5 % run-out, and the run is marked PASS. A
run-away check (overshoot of the hold point, or v_max over the rule) should be added to the criteria.
Where it starts sliding: with the empty robot nose first on wet leaves:
  • a hold fails between 9 and 10 %;
  • driving down at 0.15 m/s with a person stepping in fails between 10 and 11 %, and at 11 % the robot cannot

7. Verdict
  • On the lane as modelled (DEM profile, 8.4 % max), the rev 5.1 settings pass every lane test we re-ran at grip
    0.22, matching their numbers closely.
  • The stop-distance claims mostly reproduce. One does not: the 8.5 % empty wet-leaf point, which the
    speed rule is built on, comes out at 170 mm here against their 120 mm. Both stand-in and theirs agree that 9 %
    gives 172-174 mm and 10 % slides.


                                                            4
  • The real margin is small. The empty robot on wet leaves goes from “fine” to “slides” between 8.5 and 10 %.
    Any of these takes the stop past the foam:
       – a 25 m stretch at 10 % or more, which a 25 m DEM cannot resolve;
       – grip 0.18 instead of 0.22;
       – meeting the slope at level speed.
    12 % is a runaway that hits the person.
Recommendations:
  • survey the hill (the steepest 10 m and 25 m), and repeat the X8n/X9n checks at the surveyed grade;
  • set the empty-robot speed limit from that survey, with margin;
  • slow down from map knowledge or an IMU before the brow, not at it;
  • keep the castor-brake or tail-first option open for the empty robot;
  • send the rev5 mass table so that the 8.5 % point can be settled.
