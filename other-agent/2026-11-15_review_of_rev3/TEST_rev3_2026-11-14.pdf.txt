  BinRobo revision 3 - independent re-test (mechanical gate and lane),
                             MuJoCo 4.0.1
                                     Tester report, 14-15 November 2026




1. Verdict
The revision 3 results reproduce. Their own scorer, run on my re-runs, gives the same score: 8 of 9 gate
criteria pass. Only G9 (weight: 152.2 kg against the 120 kg limit) fails. All 180 gate pick-ups succeed,
with no false holds and no bins knocked over. All 24 stress cases pass. The lane run P1 uses 15.39 Wh, the same as
theirs.
The passes are narrower than the headline suggests. The gate passes because of four modelling switches and one
unconfirmed hardware number:
  • The castor tyre is the main weakness. On G3 “step both”, the pass/fail result flips with castor contact
    width. A 30 mm strip passes, a 36 mm strip stalls, and 50 mm passes again. The cold-eye estimate puts a real
    flat 50 mm tyre at an effective 31-35 mm, which is right on that edge. The slippery hill start has the same
    problem. The 24 mm strip passes only on the filtered castor rule (raw minimum 48.8 N, below the 50 N limit).
    The flat 50 mm tread fails outright: 6.7 deg at 152 kg and 8.75 deg at 166.8 kg.
  • The sharp 40 mm step needs 80 N m of wheel torque, not 60. The drive supplier has not confirmed
    80 N m. At 60 and 70 N m the robot stalls. 80 N m climbs at 152 kg and at 156.6 kg (the cold-eye central
    mass). At 166.8 kg (+10 %), 80 N m also stalls (new failure). 100 N m climbs.
  • Lane gaps (not run by them at 152 kg):
       – Open 60 mm pothole: stalls at 60 N m after 417 s stuck. Passes at 80 N m, at both 152 and 166.8 kg.
       – Open 40 x 200 mm trench: stalls at both 60 and 80 N m.
       – So “pothole filled” and “trench covered” stay mandatory site rules at the gate torque.
  • The new seating check (X_POSTHOLD) changes nothing in the gate set. It fired once in 180
    pick-ups. With the check off, that same trial still succeeds. No pick-up depends on it.
  • Bookkeeping error in their gate file: the G8 hand-calc block in their gate_B2F__r3.json was read from
    a stale file for a 93.2 kg robot, and states 36.5 deg. Their own revision 3 file gives 31.9 deg (castor lift-off 26.0
    deg). G8 still passes (limit 20 deg).
Overall: as a simulation result, revision 3 is reproducible and honest. As a design, it has no margin on the step, the
castor or the actuator (the lift sits at its 3 kN cap on the 40 mm steps). It also fails G9 by 32 kg. The 80 N m step
fix holds at 156.6 kg but not at 166.8 kg, so it stops working somewhere near the top of the realistic 152-162 kg
range (162 kg itself not run).

4. What their switches hide
4.1 Castor tread width (X_CAST_W=0.012, a 24 mm contact strip)

  • The 36 mm stall is real in the model. It repeats at dt 0.25 ms (step40_both_dt025_w018), so it is not a
    time-step artefact.
  • Swivel offset (trail) also flips results.
       – 24 mm strip with a 35 mm trail: step both stalls (0.45 m).
       – 50 mm strip with a 65 mm trail: step both stalls (1.34 m).
  • Where real tyres sit. The cold-eye review (M-7) puts a real flat 50 mm tread at an effective half-width of
    15.6-17.3 mm, i.e. a 31-35 mm strip. That is the gap between the last pass (30 mm) and the first stall (36
    mm). A crowned tyre is about 8-10 mm, i.e. a 16-20 mm strip, and passes.
  • Conclusion. Their 24 mm choice is physically plausible: it lies between a real flat tyre and a real round one.
    But the G3 and S7 verdicts depend on castor parameters nobody has measured. Recommendation: specify
    a crowned or round-profile castor tyre, then measure its swivel torque under about 440 N.
  • X_CAST_SHOULDER=0.003 is practically inert. S7 and the lane P5d/P5h runs are identical to the plain 24
    mm strip; only the transient step-both lift peak changes. Their “shouldered wheel” spot checks therefore add
    no evidence.

4.2 Seating check (X_POSTHOLD=0.030) - counterfactual
  • Fired once in 180 gate pick-ups: ESE 240 CL, seed 303, trial 25. The first reading was 43.7 mm, so the
    bin was set down, the robot re-docked, and the second reading was 13.6 mm (success, 121 s).
  • Same trial with the check off (MC_IDX=25, B2F_grab_ESE240CL_r3noph_s303_i25.json): success first
    time. Seated, keeper on the bar, comb nib 25.2 mm off-centre, wheels 92 mm clear, 68 s.
  • Full check-off sets for seeds 201/202/203 (_r3noph): identical, 90/90. Seeds 303-503 with the check off were
    not re-run, because the check never fires there except in trial 25 and the runs are deterministic.
  • Result: 0 of 180 pick-ups pass only because of the seating check.
  • False holds by comb-offset band:
       – No trial is 50 mm or more off-centre (and so none at 80 mm), with the check on or off: 0 false holds.
       – Maximum |nib_y| is 23.9 mm with the check on and 25.2 mm in the check-off trial.

4.3 Docking retries (X_DOCK_TRIES=3, X_DOCK_DYMAX=0.025)
   • 23 of the 180 “successes” needed at least one back-off and re-approach before the lift. Trial 303/25 needed two.

     not run.

4.5 Lift actuator
On the 40 mm step gate runs, the lift force peaks at its 3,000 N cap:
   • G3 one: 2,995 N; G3 both: 3,000 N.
   • At 156.6 kg, step one: 3,000 N.
   • In the open trench the lift sits on the cap and more wheel torque does not help.
So the actuator has no margin (cold-eye M-3).

6. Mass sensitivity (+10 % = 166.8 kg; 156.6 kg)
  • Gate items at 166.8 kg:
      – G3 step one/both pass (lift peak 2,630 / 2,486 N).
      – Climb grip 0.22 passes; e-stop fwd/rev pass.
      – Release CL up 12 % passes; 10/10 pick-ups.
      – Tip hand calc 31.9 deg.
      – S7 hill start: 24 mm strip 1.3 deg (raw castor minimum 47.6 N, below 50); flat tread 8.75 deg FAIL.
  • Lane at 166.8 kg: P1 passes (+0.7 % energy). The sharp step needs 100 N m.
  • At 156.6 kg: all the same gate items pass, and S7 at 24 mm gives 1.4 deg with a castor minimum of 72 N.
  • In short: mass mainly hurts the step torque and the flat-tread hill start. The gate items run here survive
    +10 %.
