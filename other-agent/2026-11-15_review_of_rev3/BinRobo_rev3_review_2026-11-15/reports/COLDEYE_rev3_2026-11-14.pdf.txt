Verdict
A real step forward, honestly reported, but still not a build basis. Revision 3 fixes the lift top end, the floating
parts and the weight table, and the reply admits its own errors plainly. Our checks reproduce their weight (152.19 kg)
row for row and their stop position (q 335.1 mm) to 0.5 mm. What remains is mostly in what nobody checked yet: who
protects a person in front of the robot, what the actuator does at its own end of travel, fatigue at the new welds, and
whether the gate limits they want moved (120 kg, 50 Wh, 60 N m) are being moved for a reason.
Scope: drawings, design and claims only. No MuJoCo or SITL run here (the sibling streams rev3-mech and rev3-sitl do
that). Every number below comes from OpenCascade booleans on their own model script, from hand calculations in
calcs_rev3.py, or from a page cited in the text. ASSUMED marks a value with no datasheet behind it.

Blocker
B-1. The front bumper is a trip switch, not a person stop; lowering has no safety function
Evidence (calcs_rev3.json “bumper”; Fig 5):
  • The bar slides 15 mm on sprung pins.
  • The robot’s own G4 stop from 0.8 m/s takes 0.23 m on the 12 % slope, about 1.4 m/s2.
  • With 0.05-0.1 s from contact to braking, the robot travels 0.17 to 0.31 m after contact: 11 to 20 times the bar
    travel.
  • Kinetic energy is 49 J empty and 84 J with a full bin. After the bar bottoms out, all of it goes into whatever the
    bar is pressing.
  • The bar only stops the robot within its own travel below about 0.15 m/s.
  • The usual rule for bumper-type person detection on driverless vehicles (ISO 3691-4, EN 1525 before it) is that the
    compliant travel must cover the stopping distance. That rule is not met. The design never states which standard it
    targets; sheet 15 says only “PL d”.
Lowering:
  • The 4 kN screw actuator drives the carriage and bin down with up to its full force (about 5.6 kN at the 4 A limit
    on sheet 15).
  • Sheet 15 has no safety function for this: no lower-only-when-clear, no force limit on the way down, and the down
    switch is a single channel.
  • A foot under the bin wheels or the comb at set-down is exposed.
What it needs:
  • Either about 0.3 m of compliant bumper (foam or a deflecting skirt) at 0.8 m/s, or a speed limit of about 0.15 m/s
    whenever people can be present.
  • Or a safety-rated presence sensor (a safety laser scanner, about EUR 1.5-3k) with the bumper kept as a last resort.
  • A lowering function: a down-force current limit and a stop on the comb or pad ToF.
  • The dock contacts sit on the bumper face. Docking presses the bar, so the bumper trips during docking. Muting it
    is a safety function of its own and is not designed.
This is shared with B2-F, whose bumper bar has the same concept. It blocks unattended operation, not a bench prototype.

B2-F, audited from our own CAD (cad_B2F_vol.py -> solids_B2F.json):
  • Carriage: lean build 24.9 kg (10 mm 640 comb at the S12 8 mm, 12 mm aluminium plate, RHS pad arm), 35.2 kg

  • Guards, flares, rollers and brackets: 5.6 kg against 1.60 kg, +4.0 kg.
  • Rail drop plates +0.8 kg, HDPE +0.7 kg, fasteners +1.5 to 2.5 kg, actuator -1.05 kg.
  • With the same bought-in corrections as revision 3, B2-F with S12 is 135 to 158 kg. Without S12, whose 2 mm

Where 120 kg came from. It is the concept-gate figure from the G6 review (“mass <= 120 kg”), set so that concept
B stayed comparable to A’ at 115.8 kg (REVIEW_G6_coldeye.md, CONCEPT_GATE_G6.md). It is a comparison gate, not a
physical requirement. The reply is right that no physical basis was given.
What the weight still costs:
  • Energy: +7 Wh per trip.
  • The sharp 40 mm step: lost at 60 N m.
  • Tip margin: down to 31.9 deg, which is still large.
  • Hand recovery: about 390 N to push 152 kg plus a full bin up 12 % (340 N at 120 kg). That is a two-person job
    either way.
Fix:
  • Replace G9 with functional criteria: SOC, step, stop and tip, all already in the gate, plus a cap on a weighed
    prototype.
  • 160 kg leaves no margin over our 152-162 kg range; 165 kg would be honest.
  • Apply the same criteria to B2-F.

M-2. Side rail: SF 2.04 rests on the tyre model; the welded corner plate is a fatigue detail
Reproduced:
  • 174.4 MPa at the 3,724 N wheel load with M = F x 0.49 m and Z = 10,461 mm3 (sharp corners).
  • The load reading from their 4.0.1 runs is applied correctly. The categories are as in S12: compliant-tyre kerb and
    pothole for “edge”, the rest for “carry”.
  • We accept the 3.17 edge artefact.
Not covered:
  • The same 4.0.1 runs give 10,406 N on a rigid-tyre 40 mm step. They list it as “info only”, as S12 did. On that
    load the rail SF is 0.73 (Fig 4).
  • Excluding it is only valid if the real tyre is as soft as the 15 mm compliant-tyre model. The BOM wheel (DRV-W8)
    is a solid PU 330 mm wheel, which sits closer to the rigid model.
  • Fatigue (rough, ASSUMED spectrum):
       – Load: static drive-wheel load with a full bin 845 N, +/-50 % per bump.


                                                          3
         – Cycles: about 2.0 million in 10 years (3 bins a week, 1 bump per metre, half the distance loaded).
         – The rear corner plate is fillet-welded on the rail top face at x 450, right at the peak moment. That is roughly
           FAT 50.
         – Stress range 39.6 MPa against an allowable of 37.2 MPa, a ratio of 1.07 (fails).

M-4. Revision 3 needs welding; Brian intended a bolted DIY build
B2-F’s FAB-B2 line is “saw-cut and drilled by Brian, bolted through about 16 laser-cut plates”. Revision 3 has:
   • 4 corner plates welded over the frame joints;
   • 2 uprights welded all round (5 mm fillet) to their foot shoes;
   • 2 castor-pivot hanger plates welded to both faces of the nose cross-member;
   • 2 bumper-carrier flanges welded;
   • 4 stop brackets whose tongue is perpendicular to the plate, which means welded or machined from a block.
The reply flags this (decision 4), which is fair. Cost of a fabricator: about EUR 150-400 (ASSUMED).
Bolted equivalents exist for every item:
   • bolted end plates with crush tubes;
   • a clamp plate pair for the pivot;
   • angle brackets for the bumper carrier;
   • stop blocks machined from 40 mm bar.
This is Brian’s decision; it also feeds M-2, because a bolted corner plate is the better fatigue detail.

M-5. Safety sheet 15: right topology, not yet a PL d design
Credit: every device has its own input pair (no series chain); both contactors’ mirror contacts are in the feedback loop;
two tilt switches; pre-charge; a power diode for braking energy; lift interlocks; PL targets per function.
Still missing or wrong:
   1. No PL calculation: no MTTFd, DC, CCF or SISTEMA. Their reply says so.
   2. The safety controller with 9 monitored two-channel inputs is not chosen. The BOM still holds the used PNOZ X3
      (EUR 61). Budget about EUR 300-550.
   3. The bumper switches: BOM BMP-L8 is Omron D4N-4120, which is 1NC/1NO. Sheet 15 asks for two NC
      contacts per switch. Change the part, or use antivalent inputs.
   4. KM-L8 contactors (SW100-class DC) normally have no mirror contacts to EN 60947-4-1 Annex F, so the EDM on
      sheet 15 needs different parts. Allow up to about EUR 400.
   5. The lift driver is not chosen. Its “enable” input is not a safety function. That is acceptable only because the hard
      stops carry the load (M-3).
   6. Lowering has no safety function (B-1).
   7. Actuator holding and brake: open (M-6).
   8. The speed and standstill monitor is own-build on RP2040s: uncertified, so it needs a validation plan.
   9. Both tilt switches are the same ball type, which is a common-cause weakness. Pair one with an inclinometer or the
      flight controller’s IMU on a separate input.

M-6. Actuator data conflicts
  • Holding: the bin hangs on it after every stop. Neither supplier page gives a self-locking or static holding figure.
    Still open.
  • Ingress protection: Anodas lists IP65. Botland lists IP42 for ELB-15868, the same Elektrobim FDI 4000 N
    / 6 mm/s / 400 mm unit. IP42 will not survive Irish rain on an outdoor robot. Sheet 13 says “IP65 unit”.
  • Closed length: 620 vs 595 mm (affects M-3).
  • Botland states built-in limit switches, 3 A maximum, 50 W. There is no stall-force figure on either page.
  • Get all four values (holding, IP rating, closed length, stall force) in writing before ordering.

M-7. Castor round-profile tyre: the fail-to-pass flip comes from a contact-model parameter
The model (make_simB2hx.py) is a condim-3 cylinder, which touches the ground at its two rim edges. Spin friction per
unit mu N is the half-width: 25 mm for the flat 50 mm tread, 12 mm for the 24 mm strip.
Pressure-weighted real patches (castor_spin.py; patch sizes ASSUMED; Fig 6) give about:
  • 15.6-17.3 mm for a real flat 50 mm tread;
  • 8.3-9.7 mm for a round profile.
So the flat model overstates a real flat tyre by about 1.5x, and the 24 mm strip sits between a real flat tyre and a real
round one.
  • The 6.7 deg fail is probably pessimistic, and the 1.4 deg pass does not prove a round-profile tyre is needed.
  • A 24 mm contact width on a 200x50 crowned powerchair tyre under about 440 N is plausible.
  • The change is cheap and points the right way, but the evidence is model tuning.
  • The swivel offset is 50 mm in the model; get the real part’s figure.
Fix: bench-measure the swivel torque of the chosen castor at 450 N on wet tarmac and set the model contact width from
that. Until then, report S7 as “passes with a contact-model assumption”.

M-8. Goalpost moves: 80 N m, 65/70 Wh, 160 kg
80 N m per wheel (Fig 4, calcs_rev3.json “drive”):
  • Hardware (ASSUMED Kt 0.044 N m/A, worm efficiency 0.70): 80 N m is 1.07 N m at the motor, 27 A per motor,
    24 N m at the 32:1 gearbox output (a comparable unit is rated 60 N m max) and 0.98 kN in the 08B chain (about
    18 kN breaking).
  • That is within the RoboClaw’s 30 A continuous rating. Rated continuous torque is about 50 N m at the wheel, so
    80 N m is a few-seconds rating.
  • Both wheels plus electronics draw about 56 A, above the 50 A slow main fuse (fine for seconds) and near the 60 A
    BMS. So it needs a stall timeout.
  • But: 60 N m was the frozen common term in both designs’ models (simB2.py: “60 N m = frozen common term”),
    and 80 was run on one case only.
  • Re-run G2, G3, G4 and the S5/S6 loads at 80, for B2-F as well. The kerb loads in S12 already use 80/150 N m.
Trip energy 50 -> 65/70 Wh:
  • Their 57.8-64.3 Wh is exactly the mass effect: our rev2 slope of 0.207 Wh/kg predicts 59.6 Wh at 152 kg.
  • 50 Wh is a per-trip proxy (C6). The physical rule, end SOC of at least 20 % after a three-bin evening, passes:
      – 50 % at 64.3 Wh with a new 480 Wh pack;
      – 31 % with an aged (80 %) pack at 0 C (85 %).
  • Acceptable if C6 is re-stated as that SOC rule at aged and cold capacity, and the energy guard’s budget is raised to
    match.
160 kg: see M-1.
Verdict on M-8: these are goalpost moves, each defensible because the functional criterion behind it still passes. Brian
decides, and the same limits must then apply to B2-F.

  • m-1. Pad springs (Fig 7):
       – The geometry is consistent: 4.5 wire, D 26.5, 14.5 active coils give 15 N/mm. Free length 134 mm, fitted 124
          mm (150 N each), 79 mm at full stroke (825 N each, 1.65 kN per pair), 768 MPa (Wahl).
       – Solid length is 74.3 mm, so 4.7 mm of clearance, below the EN 13906-1 minimum of 9.9 mm.

          needed. Seat the springs on spigots or in pockets.

       – The softer pad we suggested (0.6 kN at full stroke) is still untested.
  • m-2. The actuator body sits 4 mm into its base-bracket back plate (6.8 cm3). Their clash check allows this pair.
  • m-3. Mast foot shoe. Their SF 5.38 adds plastic capacities of the shear and tension bolts. In clearance holes the
    two tension bolts take the 1,018 N m first: 22 kN each, SF 2.44. That still passes. Specify preload, and crush
    tubes in the 50x50x5 member.
  • m-4. Motor plate. The 6 mm drive plate under a sideways tyre load has SF 1.18 at 1 kN (2.37 at 500 N; plate
    alone, ASSUMED load). It is not in S12 or in struct_r3. The same applies to B2-F. Add a gusset or show that the
    flange carries the load.
  • m-5. Sheet 13 says the rail safety factor is 2.2; the calculation says 2.04.
  • m-6. The comb clears the side guards by 8 mm a side (700 mm comb, 716 mm between guards). That is tight for a
    self-aligning comb.
  • m-7. Comb loads. The reply says comb loads “stay about the same” on 4.0.1, but the 8,148 N is B2-F’s keeper-on

    drawings, hole patterns, weld symbols, pin-to-pin length, tolerances or bolt torques. “Concept, not for manufacture”
