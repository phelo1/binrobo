             BinRobo improved version - ArduPilot SITL lane suite
                        Like-for-like re-run of the B2-F 60-run suite, 2026-11-13




Result
Improved version: 53 / 60 PASS. B2-F baseline: 52 / 60 PASS. Net change +1, from five flipped runs
(3 gained, 2 lost). No scenario changed its failure mechanism. Every flip happens in a run that sits on a known
threshold race in B2-F. None of them is evidence that the improved hardware fixes or causes anything.
The improved version is B2-F plus a fixed pad, 350 mm lift travel on a mast 60 mm taller, and the nose castors on a
rocker beam. Footprint and bumper are unchanged. Of these changes, the kinematic SITL can represent only the
longer lift and set-down times and the extra mass. See Section 5.
Scoring used the frozen CRITERIA_B2F_SITL.md (sha256 586b61b8...efdefd, unchanged) with the same
scorer logic as B2-F. Queue: 20 scenarios x seeds 7, 11 and 23. Binary: ArduPilot Rover 4.6.2 SITL
(sitl/bin/ardurover_aarch64).

Findings:
   • None of the known B2-F bugs was touched. The s3a 140 L home offset, the s1b/s3b lidar line-up and
     the s4b lateral sign are all unchanged. The comparison is like-for-like.
   • Mass is 95.74 kg, slightly above the briefed “about 94-95 kg”. The value comes from
     revG/sim/cg/h2/mass_imp_nosecut.json (96.94 kg improved minus the 1.2 kg nose cut). In this model
     mass affects only the energy and traction terms. It changes the per-trip energy by roughly 1 Wh and does not
     affect any pass/fail result.
   • The lift-time scaling is a conservative upper bound. The whole B2-F lift and set-down time is scaled
     by 1.4, as if all of it were actuator stroke. A fixed pad would in fact remove some toggle time.


                                                        1
  • The castor model is NOT changed. The world keeps the 5 % turning-scrub castor term. A rocker
    beam has no representation in a flat, kinematic, rigid-body world, so the “castor model” change in the brief
    amounts to no code change here. The fixed pad is not modelled either: the companion still sends the pad
    servo command, and its time is folded into the scaled lift time.
  • DI-2 is a code change, but a behaviour-neutral one. In an uninterrupted run, the withdraw phase
    already holds the bin name in a local variable. The fallback only runs when a run is restored from a checkpoint
    inside that phase, where B2-F would otherwise throw a KeyError. It is not one of the B2-F performance bugs.
    The seed-7 runs up to s3a (finished 17:41 UTC) predate the edit (17:44 UTC). That makes no difference unless
    a resume hit that phase.

4. Why each change happened
s1b seeds 11 and 23 (FAIL -> PASS), s3b seed 23 (FAIL -> PASS), s3b seed 11 (PASS -> FAIL).
These four runs differ only in the C8 pre-grab gate (lateral <= 0.05 m, yaw <= 3 deg), which comes from the known
lidar line-up weakness. Across the whole suite, the gate’s lateral error sits right on the threshold in both versions:

                                gate attempts    misses     median lateral     p90 lateral   max
                   B2-F         24               9          0.028 m            0.077 m       0.082 m
                   improved     25               7          0.024 m            0.057 m       0.079 m


Any change to the timeline moves the robot through the same seeded RTK and odometry noise at different moments,
and that is enough to flip a 0.05 m gate. Examples:


                                                            2
  • s1b seed 11: B2-F -0.061 / -0.079 m (MISS, MISS); improved -0.007 m (OK).
  • s3b seed 11: B2-F needed a retry on brown (-0.060 MISS, then OK); improved missed black twice (-0.068,
    -0.057).
The longer lift adds about 13 s per lift and 10 s per set-down, so it is exactly such a timeline change. The line-up
algorithm and lidar model are identical. Read these flips as noise around a threshold, not as an improvement. The
miss rate drops from 9/24 to 7/25, which is within seed scatter.
s4c seed 11 (PASS -> FAIL). The expected alert was “no fix hold”. The improved run instead ended “firmware
fence hold” at t = 244.2 s. The truth robot was parked in HOLD (stationary from about 192 s, truth boundary not
breached, hedge 0.267 m). With no GNSS, the EKF fell back to DCM, and its position estimate drifted at about
0.27 m/s while the robot stood still. In the improved run the estimate reached the firmware fence polygon at 244.2 s.
That is 3 s before the companion’s 60 s no-fix timer would have raised the expected alert; in B2-F that timer won
at 247.4 s. The improved run stopped 0.08 m earlier along the lane, and its unaided estimate drifted on a slightly
different heading: about 0.5 m further in x by t = 240 s. That small offset is enough to cross the polygon first.
This is the same race in both versions, and it is a B2-F estimator and fence-logic property. It is not caused by the
hardware change.
s3a (0/3 in both). Same failure: the firmware fence hold after the hedge clip at the 140 L home offset. It now
happens about 12 s later (one longer lift), at 0.020-0.026 m clearance versus 0.012-0.071 m in B2-F.
s4b seed 23 (fail in both). Same lateral sign error, with hedge contact at 0.000 m in both.
Margins that moved but did not flip anything:
  • Energy per trip: mean 45.6 -> 46.7 Wh, max 46.7 -> 48.1 Wh. The 50 Wh C6 limit still holds, but the margin
    shrinks from about 3.3 to about 1.9 Wh. The causes are the +3.7 % mass and the longer 50 W lift-actuator
    dwell.
  • Latest dock in a full three-bin evening: 20:50 (s3b seed 23) versus 20:47 in B2-F. The 21:00 C3 limit holds
    with 10 min margin. The three lifts and three set-downs add about 70 s in total.
  • Hedge clearance in passing runs: minimum 0.192 m versus 0.196 m (limit 0.124 m).
  • C2 set-down error: maximum 0.074 m versus 0.054 m (limit 0.15 m).


5. Caveats
  • The SITL is kinematic and cannot see the castor/bumper clash. The world is a flat rigid-body
    skid-steer with a 5 % scrub term. It has no castor geometry, no rocker articulation, no suspension and no 3D
    contact. Any clash between the rocker-mounted castors and the unchanged bumper, and any benefit of the
    rocker over kerbs or bumps, is invisible here. That has to be settled in MuJoCo or CAD.
  • The taller mast and longer travel are modelled only as time. Stability with a raised bin, mast deflection and
    the height of the lidar self-occlusion sector are unchanged from B2-F.
  • The fixed pad is not modelled. The pad servo is still commanded, and its time is folded into the 1.4x scaled
    lift time, which is conservative.
  • The mass, 95.74 kg, is about 0.7 kg above the briefed 94-95 kg. The effect is on energy only.
  • With 3 seeds per scenario, the +1 difference is well inside run-to-run scatter. The honest conclusion is that
    the improved version is neutral in SITL: no new failure mechanism, and slightly thinner energy and time
    margins.
  • C8 hands off to the MuJoCo front-dock gate (90/90). The grab itself is not re-checked here for the new lift
    geometry.
