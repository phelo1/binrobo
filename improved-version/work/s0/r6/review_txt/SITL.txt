At 80 Wh, 4 runs fail for reasons other than energy:
   • 3 x “too late” (s3b, all seeds): three returns from 18:00 on collection day. Two returns end at 20:34-20:35; the companion
     refuses the third because its own estimate (81 min) would end at about 21:56, after 21:00. This is the companion’s 21:00
     deadline working as coded. Under bye-law 6 (bins removed by midnight) the third return fits with about 2 h to spare.
   • 1 x contact stop (s3a seed 7): on the second drop, just after lifting the black bin at home, the loaded robot pivoted at about
     0.30 rad/s (cap 0.13) and its body touched the parked green bin. The stop chain opened, the robot stopped. Their run of this
     seed passed. Seeds 11 and 23 completed all three drops. This is a real, if rare, collision with a bin in the yard.
The pause rule works as described for a person who is ahead and in view. It pauses, waits, sends the 2-minute message
and moves on by itself. It does not cover three cases we stress-tested:
   1. Someone stepping out 2.6 m ahead at night: contact (1 of 2 seeds). At 3.07 m: 0.50 m clearance; at 3.5 m: 1.14-1.25 m.
   2. Someone overtaking from behind or standing beside the robot is not seen (front camera only): 0.38 m and 0.48 m
      passes, no pause.
   3. The 15-minute hold never fires when the detection is intermittent (60 % per frame at night): a person standing 1,000 s
      near the lane produced 8 pauses (318, 329, 93, 65, 142, 23, 25, 10 s), the 2-minute message 3 times, and no hold, because every
      3 s gap resets the timer.
Rev 4 issues: 2 fixed (s4c alarm race, s1b s23 hedge), 1 fixed only with a switch on (trip-time check), 2 not fixed (final reverse
steers on true pose; slope speed uses the true bin mass).

Reading:
   • The rule keeps 3 m or more from someone ahead and in view from far off, as claimed.
   • Someone who appears close at night is the weak case: with 60 % per frame and two detections in a row needed, confirmation
     can take several frames. At 2.6 m that was too late once in two runs. The bump strip and stop chain then stopped the robot,
     as designed.
   • Behind and beside: no software pause at all, as their reply already says. 0.38 m from a person walking past is real.
   • The 15-min hold should run on time with someone near in the last N minutes, not on one unbroken track.
   • False detections cost time (about 5 s each) and energy (+1.9 Wh per trip at 1/min); a trip still fits under 80 Wh.

earlier than 18:00 the day before collection, and removed by midnight on collection day. The 21:00 figure was only on

9. Not run / limits
  • 4.7.x firmware not run (4.6.2 only). The s3a s7 contact stop and other differences from their runs were not investigated across
    firmware.
  • Stress runs: seed 7 (and 23 for some) only; hold15, slowwalk and phantom one run each; no stress on s3a / s3b.
  • Not simulated: a companion with a midnight return deadline; three returns started between 17:00 and 19:55; a rear camera
    or rear lidar (not in the model); a real person detector (all detection rates are assumed).
  • Their slope-speed alternatives and the lane-physics (MuJoCo) results were not re-run here; the SITL world does not model wet
    leaves.
  • Two score runs (s1b s7, s3a s7) are copies of the reproduction runs at TRIP_WH 75 / quiet hours 22:30; see section 1.
  • Seating sensor and camera yaw remain idealised in their code; the final reverse still uses truth (section 6).
