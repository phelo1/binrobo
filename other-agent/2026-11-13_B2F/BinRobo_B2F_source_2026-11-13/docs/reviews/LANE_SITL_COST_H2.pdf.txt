Bottom line. With the layout G6 uses (handles toward the road), both designs fit the lane, and both run all ten
SITL scenarios to the strict thresholds declared before the runs. Rev H2 cannot do a handle-to-lane drop at pad A:
it misses by 1 cm under both rule sets, and moving the pads 0.25 m up-lane does not help. In the lane fit B2-F has
more margin. In SITL, though, its rails come within 3 mm of the truth hedge at pad A. Neither design keeps the
0.124 m p95 localisation error clear at pad A in SITL. Pad A is the place to protect, by trimming the hedge or by
setting the pad 0.1-0.2 m off the hedge. On cost Rev H2 is about EUR 250 cheaper on both bases. That saving is
now on a like-for-like basis, after the B2 duplicate actuator was removed and four Rev H2 prices were checked.

Against the 0.124 m p95. Rev H2 with the G6 layout keeps 0.181 m, so +0.057 m above p95. That margin is set
by the withdraw at A and by the hedge at s 417 on the cmp rules. B2-F keeps 0.236 m with handles to the road and
0.175 m with handles to the lane under the b2 rules. Its 0.032 m under the cmp rules is below p95.
The 0.25 m up-lane pad move was tested on Rev H2 only. It changes nothing at the binding steps, which are
limited by the road legal boundary and the hedge laterally. It does raise the pickup “pass the bin” margin from
0.190 m to 0.318 m.


S9 Carrier SITL (kinematic, Python; ArduPilot is not in the loop)
The model is a new carrier model (work/h2lane/carrier_sitl.py) on the G6 site and G6 scenario geometry. The bin
is rigid, and there is no hitch or relay flip. The robot follows pure pursuit on its estimated pose. The localisation
error depends on the GPS zone: fix, float, or none with dead reckoning, plus lidar corridor aid. It includes the
obstacle stop, a resume after 2 s clear, a by-pass of static objects, and the G4 rule for an alternate home slot. Fixed
manoeuvre times come from revH2/sim/mission.py.
Thresholds were declared before any scored run (PROGRESS 14:27):
  • truth hedge or region margin below 0 fails;
  • overlap with a car, branch or dock fails;
  • a gap to a standing bin below 0.05 m fails;
  • a person or dog gap below 0.30 m while moving fails;
  • a placement more than 0.60 m or 20 degrees off fails;
  • the outcome must equal the declared expectation.
The code was frozen (md5 70751861) and every scored run was then repeated from scratch.

All 20 runs pass strict. The night one-bin drop uses 41.5 Wh against 45.7 Wh in mission.json, and the three-bin
drop 131 Wh against 135 Wh, which agree within 3-10 %.
During development the model found five real planning faults, now fixed (PROGRESS 14:25-15:45):
  • a withdraw that was too short;
  • no “pass the bin” step;
  • a second pivot that was never checked;
  • a reverse/forward sign error;
  • obstacle detection that looked only at the end of the stopping distance.
Every pre-freeze output is kept in sitl_out/dev/. Not covered: about 50 G6 hitch, relay and dock-fault scenarios,
which do not apply to a carrier. Bin and rail contact on the approach also falls outside this model, because that is
MuJoCo territory.


S13 Energy and motor current
The evening is three bins at 0.5 C at night, taken from the SITL energy with the mass scaled per pack. A route
cross-check (EU-DEM 25 m) agrees within 7 % on driving energy.

The new-AGM Rev H2 needs a recharge between the drop evening and the pickup evening. B2-F’s LFP pack cannot
be charged below 0 C without the dock pack heater.
For current, on a steady 12 % climb at 0.5 m/s with a 110 kg bin, Rev H2 draws 24.0 A on the new drive and 25.4
A on the donor drive, against the RoboClaw’s 30 A continuous rating. Accelerating at 0.25 m/sˆ2 that rises to 28-30
A. Those figures use the conservative Kt of 1.0 N m/A implied by params.py. The back-EMF Kt gives 16-17 A.
B2-F draws 12.3 A. The real lane’s steepest grade is 7.9 % over 10 m (EU-DEM). The 12 % case comes from SRTM
and is the project’s design case. The donor motors are still unmeasured, so test B5 is needed.
