# BinRobo improved version: revision 6.1

27 November 2026. For Brian and for the other agent.

Brian's instruction after revision 6 was "do all the things you think are best", and then to put the project in a GitHub repository. This note says what I decided, what I changed, what I tested and what I found. No new review came in between; revision 6.1 follows from my own list of open points.

Hardware is unchanged from revision 6. The changes are in the software and the tests. Sheet 18 of the drawings is updated; the other sheets carry the new revision number only.

## Summary for Brian


**What I took as decided.** These were the "decisions for you" of revision 6. I have gone with my own recommendation on each.

1. **Speed.** Fit the 24-tooth sprockets. Start with the fast cruise off (0.4 m/s at most, touch-safe everywhere, 66 to 75 minutes a trip). Switch it on (36 to 43 minutes) once tests 7 to 9 and 13 have passed on the real robot. There are now two settings files for exactly this. One thing to know: with the fast cruise off, three pick-ups on one day only fit before 21:00 if the first starts by 17:15 (section 4.2).
2. **Third lidar and bigger computer.** In. They are in the parts list and the cost.
3. **Bins home by 21:00.** Stays.
4. **Leaf-season switch.** On from October to December until the hill is measured. That means no pick-up trips in those months for now.

**What is new in 6.1**

- **A bad lidar no longer stops the robot.** Until now the lidar in my tests was ideal. I wrote a test that spoils every scan: returns go missing, false ones appear, the hedge is ragged and sways. With the revision 6 software the robot could not even leave the yard. After six changes it completes a drop-off and a pick-up at both test levels, in the same 36 to 42 minutes as on a clean one, with no false stop in the lane (section 2).
- **Two gaps in people avoidance are closed.** Someone who walks up and then stands quite still near the robot now holds a turn for as long as they stand there (before: a few seconds). And someone close to the hedge is now found when they are 0.2 m clear of it (before: 0.3 to 0.5 m).
- **It reacts sooner.** The stand-still command comes 0.4 s after someone steps out of the hedge, on average. In revision 6 it was 0.5 s.
- **Everything ran on one frozen code.** 210 simulated encounters with people, the 60-mission suite, and 32 further trips. In revision 6 the tests were spread over several stages of the code; that was my own first open point.
- **The 60 missions:** 48 of 60 by the B2-F script as it stands (revision 6: 45) and 60 of 60 with the bye-law windows and a realistic trip budget (revision 6: 57). All 12 full missions complete with no contact. But the robot now stops about twice at every road-end turn of a pick-up, for 3 to 4 s, with nobody there (section 4.1).
- **With the start settings (fast cruise off)** one run in 48 ends with a touch: a child running out 0.4 m in front of the nose reaches the robot as it comes to rest. Nobody else is touched.
- **What did not get better.** With the fast cruise on, someone who appears less than about 1 m in front of the nose still meets a robot that is braking. That is the price of the fast cruise and it is why you start without it. The reviewers' case (1 m in front of the nose) sits on the edge at the full 1.0 m/s: in four runs at that speed the robot stopped 0.07 to 0.23 m short three times, and once the walker brushed the nose corner as the robot came to rest (section 3.2).
- **Hardware and cost are unchanged:** EUR 4,241 to 5,138 all-in.

**Two things only you can do,** and both are still open:

- **Measure the hill** (a 2 m board and a phone, half an hour; test 1 of the revision 6 note).
- **Say whether the mapped lane is the right one** (635 m from the house to the public road).

## 2. A worse lidar and a real hedge

The revision 6 note said the lidar in my simulation was an ideal scan with a little noise, and that a test with dropouts and false returns was "the test the lane watch most needs". I asked the other agent whether their model could do it. No answer has come, so I wrote it myself.

### 2.1 What the test does

A switch in the test runner (`X_LID_BAD`) spoils every scan of all three lidars before the software sees it. Two levels:

| What is spoiled | Stands for | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Any return missing | rain, wet leaves, shallow angles | 15 % | 30 % |
| A return from a person or animal missing, on top of that | dark, wet clothing | 30 % | 60 % |
| A 30-degree sector blank for 0.3 s | glare, a splash on the window | once in 50 s | once in 10 s |
| False returns in a scan, anywhere from 0.2 to 6 m | rain drops, insects, blown leaves | 2 | 6 |
| Hedge returns come from inside the hedge, by up to about | a hedge is not a wall | 0.2 m | 0.4 m |
| Hedge sways | wind | 0.03 m | 0.08 m |
| Share of the hedge with a spray standing 0.1 to 0.2 m out into the lane | brambles | 2 % | 5 % |

So a person gives back about 60 % of the rays that hit them at level 1 and 28 % at level 2.

These figures are my guesses. Level 1 is meant as a wet night. Level 2 is meant to be worse than anything the real lidar should do: if it is that bad, the lidar is the wrong one. What the real lidar does on this lane is test 9 on the built robot, and nothing here replaces it.

### 2.2 What it found

With the revision 6 software the robot could not leave the yard. Both level-1 trips ended with "path blocked" after 4 and 7 minutes.

| What the test showed | Cause | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Both trips ended "path blocked" in the yard | The protective field came from B2-F unchanged and stopped for any single return in the path. One rain drop was enough | Every scan is cleaned first. A return counts only if a neighbouring ray (four on each side) shows about the same range, or if the three scans before each had a lone return at the same place |
| Then 53 to 77 protective-field stops in a trip | A false return next to the hedge had the hedge as its "neighbour". And the field allowed the hedge only 0.25 m of depth | The protective field acts on two scans running. The hedge band is 0.35 m |
| At level 2, 29 to 47 stops for false returns within 0.3 m of the nose, and my own near field of revision 5 tripped three times in two minutes | Pairs of false returns close to the lidar look like neighbours | The near field also needs two scans running. Lone returns must repeat in three scans, within 0.08 m plus 5 % of the range |
| A drop-off ended "pad not clear" at the road | The wait for the pad counted raw returns more than 0.4 m off the map | It counts lane-watch objects, which need two looks |
| A 34 s stop by the house for a patch of false returns | The walker rule took a patchy cluster that shifted for a walker | A walker must be compact (0.8 m at most) and must have moved a full metre |
| The first filter halved the detection of a person who returns one ray in four | It asked for a neighbour within two rays | Four rays each side |

Each row was found by a run, changed and run again. The runs before each change are in the pack (`sitl/before_fixes`).

### 3.2 What the table shows

- **Start settings (fast cruise off): one touch in 48 runs.** A child running out 0.4 m in front of the nose reached the robot as it came to rest, in one run of three. In the other two the child reached it within 0.1 s of its stopping. The revision 6 note called this case "on the edge even at the slow speed", and it still is. Nobody else is touched.
- **Fast cruise on: 10 touches in 81 runs, in four cases.**
  - A walker 0.4 m in front of the nose: 3 of 3, at up to 0.03 m/s (revision 6: up to 0.12).
  - A running child 0.4 m in front of the nose: 3 of 3, at 0.21 to 0.67 m/s (revision 6: the same).
  - The new case, a walker who sets off straight at the robot's side when it is already beside them: 3 of 3, at up to 0.06 m/s. The robot was braking; they walked into its side.
  - The reviewers' case, 1.0 m in front of the nose: 1 of 3, at 0.008 m/s. See below.
- **The reviewers' case.** In two runs the robot stands 0.46 to 0.56 m clear. In the third, the one where it was doing the full 1.0 m/s, it gave the stand-still command 0.39 s after the walker's first step and came to rest with its nose 0.05 m short of their line. The walker is 0.5 m wide, so they brushed the nose corner 1.8 s after setting off, with the robot at 8 mm a second. The simulation counts a contact when the robot is moving at more than 5 mm a second. In revision 6 the same run ended the same way a moment later: the walker reached the same corner as the robot stopped, under that mark, so it was not counted. It is the same event for the person. With every run at 1.0 m/s the robot stopped 0.07 to 0.23 m short of the walker in 3 of 3. So at the full fast cruise this case sits on the edge: three clear and one brush in the four runs at 1.0 m/s.
- **It reacts sooner than revision 6.** Time from the person's first step to the stand-still command, for the four cases where someone walks out of the far hedge (12 runs): 0.41 s on average and 0.51 s at most. Revision 6: 0.53 s on average and 0.71 s at most. At the start settings: 0.41 s and 0.51 s (revision 6: 0.50 s and 0.61 s). The proud rule does this: a person leaving the hedge counts as an object a step earlier. Tables: `tables/react_r61.json`, `react_start_r61.json`.
- **Every run at 1.0 m/s** (`tables/stress_v10_r61.md`): the walker 0.4 m ahead is touched at 0.07 to 0.22 m/s (revision 6: up to 0.33), the running child 0.4 m ahead at 0.68 to 0.86 m/s (revision 6: up to 0.86). From 1.0 m and more nobody is touched.
- **The walker near the far hedge** (new cases): the camera finds them 4.0 to 4.4 m ahead and the robot stands until they have passed. At the start settings they then stopped for 30 s about 3 m from the robot, behind it and against the far hedge, and it stood for all 40 s. With the fast cruise on, the robot had come further up the lane and their stopping point lay 9 m behind it, so that run tested nothing new. I moved the stopping point and ran it again: they stood 0.9 to 1.3 m from the robot, against the far hedge, for 30 s, and it stayed in the firmware hold the whole time, 40 s in all.
- **Things left in the lane.** The rule is unchanged. A box well clear of the path is passed at 0.2 m/s after 20 s, except in one fast run of three, which ended with a path-blocked alert (revision 6: two of three). A box in a bend close to the path ends "path blocked" every time. That costs a walk down the lane, not safety.

### 3.3 At a turn on the spot

Five complete drop-offs in which someone walks up at a turn (`tables/turn_people_r61.json`). The first three are the tests of revision 6. The last two are new: the person stops 2.3 m from the turning centre, out of the camera's view, and stands quite still for 40 s.

| Case | Revision 6.1 |
|---|---|
| Road end, from the road side, before the turn | The camera finds them at 4.8 m. The robot stands for 43 s, until they have gone. Never turning with them inside 3 m |
| Road end, from the road side, during the turn | The same: found at 4.8 m, stood 43 s |
| Road end, from the house side, behind the robot. They keep 0.45 m from the hedge | The lidars report them 0.1 s after they cross the 3 m line, at 2.8 m (revision 6: anywhere between 2.9 and 2.0 m, up to 1.4 s late). | New: road end, from the house side; they stop 2.3 m from the turning centre and stand for 40 s | Found at 2.9 m. The robot stood for 53 s, until they had walked on. All five trips completed. The walker in these tests does not look, and in three of them they walk into the corner of the standing robot, because their line passes where its nose had come to rest. The robot had been in the firmware hold for 5 s or more by then.

The rule at a turn is still weaker than in the lane, on purpose: the robot must be able to turn beside parked bins and a parked car. What 6.1 closes is the person who walked up and then stands still. What stays open is someone who was already standing there, out of the camera's view, before the robot arrived: beyond the circle the nose sweeps (1.75 m from the axle) they do not hold a turn. With a bin on board the nose corner moves at 0.36 m/s at most in a turn, inside the touch-safe speed, with the switch strip round the corner. When a turn is cut short the robot kicks back for a moment at up to 0.5 m/s (section 4.1).

### 3.4 What it still cannot do

- **Someone who appears right in front of a fast robot.** Under about 1 m in front of the nose at the fast cruise, the robot is still braking when they meet. Figures above. With the fast cruise off this is down to the running child at 0.4 m, at standstill speed.
- **Someone who walks into its side while it passes at the fast cruise.** New case, above. The sides have no switch strip. The robot is at 0.06 m/s or less when they arrive.
- **Someone with their back against the hedge.** Within about 0.1 m of the hedge face a standing person is still taken for hedge (revision 6: 0.3 to 0.5 m). The protective field covers the robot's own path.
- **Someone already standing near a turn before the robot arrives,** outside the nose circle and out of the camera's view (section 3.3).
- **Low things.** The lidars sit 0.30 m above the ground. A cat, a lying dog or a ball is seen only by the camera, then felt by the nose strip.
- **The camera sees forward only.** Beside and behind, the robot has lidar alone. The rear camera is still not in the simulation.
- **A lidar as bad as level 2.** The robot still completes its trips, but a child running out 1 m ahead was touched at 0.18 m/s in one run of three (section 2.4).
- **All sensor figures are assumed.** Section 2 widens what was assumed; it does not measure anything.

## 5. Tried and dropped

Four things were tried and did not go in. The runs are in the pack, so nobody needs to try them again.

- **A turn-rate limit in the firmware** (`ATC_STR_RAT_MAX` 13 degrees a second). The revision 6 note said this would hold every turn at 0.22 rad/s and that I had not tested it. I have now: a drop-off and a pick-up with the limit set. The highest rate was 0.31 rad/s for 0.1 s, on the approach to a bin, against 0.28 rad/s without the limit. The limit caps the rate the firmware asks for. These peaks are the drive overshooting what was asked, so the limit does nothing for them. It is not in the settings. The cure is tuning on the real drive (open point 4). Runs: `sitl/r61_ratmax`.
- **Finding a person by the jump in range** between neighbouring lidar rays, as a second way to tell someone from the hedge behind them. On a clean hedge it worked. On a ragged hedge it reported two false "people" in every scan. Dropped; the proud rule (section 1, row 2) does the job with no false stop in a clean trip. The patch is kept as `software/patches/p2_fg_NOT_USED.py`.
- **Judging who is a walker while the robot itself is moving.** The first version of the walker rule did this. Hedge ends and wall corners then "walked" as the robot drove past them, and it stopped in a clean lane at 63 to 64 m and at 408 m, for up to 20 s. Now a thing only counts as a walker if it moved while the robot stood still. Runs: `sitl/before_fixes/r61_stand_c_walk_v1`, `r61_stand_y_walk_v1`.
- **The proud rule in its first two forms.** With no limit on width, a stretch where the lane narrows counted as something standing proud of the hedge: false stops at 73 to 86 m. With the hedge face taken from one side only, a hedge end on a bend did the same at 326 to 332 m under a degraded fix. The rule now needs a compact thing (0.8 m wide at most) and a face held from both sides. Runs: `sitl/before_fixes/r61_plain_proud_v1`, `r61_plainf_proud_one_sided`.
