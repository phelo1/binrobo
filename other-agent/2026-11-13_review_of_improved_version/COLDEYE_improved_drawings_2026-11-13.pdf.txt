  Cold-eye review: BinRobo improved-version drawing set (B2-F + fixed
                     pad, 350 mm lift, castor rocker)


                                                13 November 2026


Verdict
Good concept set, not a build basis yet. Both clash claims in the other agent’s notes are correct, and I reproduced
them from the CAD solids. The drawn version still has 3 Blockers:
  1. The castors still hit the front bumper when they swivel forward. The other agent flagged this as an open issue,
     but it happens on every reversing dock.
  2. No 4 kN FDI actuator fits 350 mm of travel in the drawn mast. The 400 mm unit can also overrun into the top
     tie.
  3. The S12 structural fixes are not in the drawn version. This is carried over from B2-F, and the drawn 120 mm
     rail-block pitch makes it worse.
There are also 7 Major findings: looms through moving parts, an incomplete e-stop and fusing chain, a finger gap, the
costs of the fixed pad, false holds, mass, and the BOM. Each finding below gives its evidence and a fix.
Scope: geometry and drawing review only. No MuJoCo or SITL was run. All clash numbers come from Open-
Cascade boolean intersections. The inputs were the other agent’s unchanged cad_B2F_src.py (identical to our
work/revB2F/cad_B2F.py, checked with diff) and their build_model.py changes, posed with their rec_B2F.npz
(improved version, lift q = 0 and 289.5 mm at carry). Scripts and raw outputs are in /workspace/work/coldeye_imp/
(clash.py, env.py, lift.py, roll.py, loomchk.py, hand.py, plus the JSON results).

1b. Castors swivelled forward vs the front bumper: CONFIRMED, and worse with the
rocker (Blocker B-1)
With the castor swivelled forward, the wheel centre is at x 1000. Its front reaches x 1100.0 at the hub and 1095.4 at z
130, the bumper’s lower edge. The bumper back face is at 1050, so the overlap is 45.4 mm. A solid boolean of the
forward wheel against the bumper strip gives 78.9 cm3 per side, over x 1050-1090, z 130-187. The envelope also cuts
the 45 deg corners (34.7 cm3, x 993-1085).
Two things make it worse than the notes say:
    • With the rocker at its stop the rising castor’s hub lifts to z 124. The wheel then fills the whole 40 mm
      depth of the bumper (x 1050-1090, up to z 213; 296 cm3).
    • It happens on every dock, not just occasionally. B2-F docks by reversing onto the bin, and get_poses.py
      notes that “the reversing approach leaves the castors swivelled”. So each dock either jams the castor or trips the
      bumper switches.
Crop: coldeye_imp_figs/crop3_s09_castor_bumper.png. Computed views: fig1_castor_bumper_side.png and
fig2_castor_envelope_plan.png.
Fix.
    • Bumper option. The back face must be at x >= 1100 + 10 mm clearance, about 60-65 mm forward. That
      makes the robot about 1,566-1,572 mm long. The nose is already 16 mm over the 1.09 m spec.
    • Castors-back option. Wheelbase 885-890 mm. The side walls must then end at x <= 725 instead of 790,
      because the envelope reaches back to x 733.
    • Decide this together with the castor size.
260 mm castor option (r 130, trail 65): the forward overlap is 95 mm, so about 50 mm more than the 200 mm
castor. The notes say about 45. It also fails in three other ways:
    • The wheel top is at 260 mm, the same as the castor mounting plate (z 260). A 260 castor needs about 300-310
      mm of build height, so the beam would rise to about 306-356 mm, 4 mm under the raised cross-member (360).
    • Its swivel radius is 197 mm. That reaches y 477, beyond the 465 mm half-width of the robot.
    • The same radius reaches inward to y 83, where it meets the ballast (+/-115), the floor tongue (+/-120) and the
      battery (+/-90, x >= 753).

3. Engineering of the three changes
3a. Fixed pad (Major M-4)
At approach (q = 0) the pad face (x 284.8) stands 0.2 mm proud of the nib tip (x 285.0). The pad springs carry a
300 N preload at 30 kN/m. A full 240 L bin rolls away with roughly 30-55 N (Crr 0.03-0.05 on 1,080 N; estimate).
What losing the retract costs:
  • Approach. The pad, not the comb, makes first contact. It pushes the bin before the nib is seated, so nib
    engagement depth under the 30 mm receiver depends on each bin’s front-wall draft. With first-arrival lateral
    errors of 15-25 mm mean and up to 47-89 mm, an off-centre push also yaws the bin.
  • Release. As the comb lowers, the pad slides about 100 mm or more down the front wall, where the EN 840
    markings are. At carry compression (7.9 mm in the sim) the spring force is about 540 N, which at mu 0.2 gives
    about 110 N of scrubbing drag.
  • The CAD cannot do what the sim assumes. The face-to-cup gap at rest is 1.2 mm against a 40 mm
    modelled stroke (C11).
The 90/90 pick-ups were on three catalogue bin models, not Brian’s bins.
Fix:
  • Design and draw a real sprung stroke of at least 45 mm.
  • Set the pad at least 10 mm behind the nib tip so the comb seats first, or keep a passive cam retract.
  • Add a test of the fixed-pad approach with the bin brake on and off, and on measured real bins.

3b. 350 mm lift (Blocker B-2)
The lift origin is z 834.9 at q = 0, 1124.4 at carry and 1184.9 at q = 350. The rod end sits at lift +135, so the space
from the base pin (about 410) to the rod end at q = 0 is about 560 mm. The FDI 4 kN 6 mm/s family as fetched
from anodas.lt on 13 Nov:
  • 400 mm stroke: L = 620 mm, IP65, internal limit switches not adjustable, no feedback, EUR 137.10.
    https://www.anodas.lt/en/fdi-linear-actuator-24v-400mm-6mm-s
  • 300 mm stroke: L = 500 mm, IP42, no feedback, EUR 150.10. https://www.anodas.lt/en/fdi-24v-linear-
    actuator-300mm-4000n-6mm-s
So the 300 mm unit fits but cannot give 350 mm of travel. The 400 mm unit is needed but is 60 mm too long.
Run to its internal end stop, it takes the carriage plate to z 1400, 30 mm into the top tie (underside 1370), and
the rail blocks to 1390, 30 mm past the rail end (1360). The taller mast does not help either problem: the closed
length is set by the bottom position, and 60 mm more of mast gives only 20 mm of overrun margin at 350.
Fix:
  • Lower the base pin at least 60 mm, to about z 350 on the front face of the mast-base cross-member. The rear
    cover (x 520-720, z 300-306) needs a cut-out.
  • Add a hard stop at 350 mm plus an external top limit switch in the actuator supply, or use an actuator with
    position feedback. Note that L5 lists a “position signal” this unit does not have.
  • Change B-0 to the 400 mm unit.
  • Cycle time: 350 mm at 6 mm/s is 58 s per lift, against 42 s for 250 mm.
Mast bending. The S12 model is M = F_comb x e_mast (e = 0.425 m) and does not depend on carriage height. The
350 mm change therefore leaves the upright foot at SF 0.68 (comb_all, 9,634 N). The 60 mm higher carry adds

less than 1% through braking inertia (110 kg x 3 m/s2 x 0.05 m = 17 N m against 2,049 N m). It is not worse, and not
fixed.
The drawn rail-block pitch is 120 mm (blocks at lift -25..35 and 95..155), but S12 assumed 200 mm. At 120 mm:
  • block load rises from 10,245 to 17,075 N, so SF on C0 drops from 2.29 to 1.37;
  • M4 rail-screw SF drops from 1.10 to 0.66.

3c. Rocker castor beam (Minor structurally; Major M-5 for the false holds)
Structure (hand.py, S12 load conventions: 3 g on the 537 N static castor sum, and a 970 N kerb strike from the drive
torque cap):
  • Pivot pin, 58 mm span, 38 mm housing: M = 113 N m, giving SF 2.28 for d16 and 3.25 for d18 (8.8 grade, 640
    MPa).
  • Hanger plates 8 x 90, loaded on their weak axis by the kerb force over a 160 mm lever: 81 MPa, SF 2.9 (S235).
  • Beam 50x40x3 RHS: SF 8.2.
  • Cross-member torsion: SF 6.7.
  • The one weak detail is the single 38 mm housing. It must carry the pitch couple from a kerb strike, 194 N m
    at a 200 mm pin height, so each bush sees 5.1 kN, about 32 MPa on a d16 x 10 bronze bush. At a 287 mm pin
    height this becomes 7.3 kN and the pin SF drops to 1.66. Fix: bushes at least 20 mm long and spaced at least 60
    mm apart, using a longer housing or one bush per hanger plate.
Roll stops. The stops touch the cross-member underside at 4.8 deg of roll. That is +/-24.2 mm at the castor, which
matches the drawn “about +/-25”. Until a stop touches, the nose rests on a single point, which cuts the static lateral
margin of the empty robot (CG x about 0.47 m) from 338 to 186 mm. Carrying, with the CG near x 0.2 m, it goes
from 372 to 291 mm.
Why 287 mm docks worse (hypothesis, not proven). I see two linked mechanisms:
  • Load transfer. A lateral castor force F_y, for example from a castor flipping during a reversing dock, acts on
    the pivot with lever h. The free rocker can only resist it by moving vertical load between the castors: dN = F_y
    x h / 0.56 m, which is 43% more at 287 mm than at 200. Asymmetric castor drag then yaws the robot during
    the final approach.
  • Sideways play. Each degree of roll lets the castor contact patches slide sideways relative to the chassis by h x
    theta. At the stop that is 15.8 mm at 200 mm and 22.8 mm at 287 mm.
The extra 7 mm of play is of the same order as the measured rise in mean first-arrival error on the clip-lid bin, from
15.8 to 24.6 mm.
How strong the evidence is. 26/30 vs 30/30 gives a Fisher two-sided p of about 0.11, and pooled 86/90 vs 90/90
about 0.12. The lateral-error shift is stronger evidence than the pass rate. Keeping 200 mm is prudent, and it is right
to make it a controlled dimension.
The false holds are a separate problem. Two runs lifted the bin 34 and 72 mm off-centre while the switches
reported a good hold. That shows the hold detection is blind to lateral seating, whatever the pivot height. Run
200 mm with more seeds, at least 100 on ESE240CL, and add a lateral-seating check, for example both comb ToFs
within a band.

3d. Looms and wiring (Major M-1, M-2)
Routing faults: C2-C4 above.
  • L2/L3 encoder drop through the drive wheel: crop crop1_s14_L2_L4_wheel.png.
  • L7 across the rails and the moving block: crop crop2_s14_L7_rails.png.
L7 needs a drag chain anchored on the mast, with one fixed hand-off point on the carriage. Its service loop must be
sized for 350 mm.
Safety chain (L10 has the two e-stop channels, and L4 and L9 carry the bumper stop loops):
  • No loom from the pod to the power box for the KM1/KM2 coils driven by the PNOZ outputs, the contactor
    EDM, or the SLS-L8 voltage sense. L1 carries power only, and BOM PC-G7 is not drawn. Without it the stop
    chain stops at the pod.
