    Cold-eye review: BinRobo revision 4 (reply to our rev 3 review, 17 A3
                           sheets, 20 Nov 2026)


                                                 21 November 2026


Verdict
Revision 4 is a serious, honest answer to our rev 3 review, and most of what it claims checks out. It is
still not a basis for unattended operation. It fixes the welds, the rails, the actuator data, the pad springs and the
end-stop layout. It also finds a real problem that our own gate never tested: the empty robot cannot hold or stop nose
first on a wet 12 % pitch. That problem applies to B2-F as well. We reproduce the weight (177.61 kg), the lift sweep, the
clash check and their stopping distances. Our hand model lands within 3-10 % of their simulation.
What is still wrong:
  • Person protection covers less than it looks. The bumper protects the middle 700 mm of a 936 mm robot,
    going forward. It does not cover pivot turns, the robot’s front corners, or reversing.
  • The lowering beams cannot work as drawn. The bin’s own wheels break them before the bin lands.
  • The lift stops can still end in a stall. Their order depends on a stroke tolerance that the LA36 data sheet does
    not support.
  • “Two bins between 18:00 and 21:00” misreads the bye-law. It comes from the window coded in the B2-F
    companion, which is our own mistake.
  • The cost is understated.
Scope: drawings, design, calculations and claims. Siblings rev4-mech and rev4-sitl run the simulations. Our numbers
come from:
  • OpenCascade checks on their own drawing model (model/sweep_r4.py, static_r4.py);
  • hand calculations in calcs_rev4.py;
  • the pages cited in the text.

B-1. The empty robot cannot hold or stop nose first on a wet 12 % pitch (theirs, confirmed,
shared with B2-F)

So a stop nose first on the assumed 12 % pitch is a slide with the wheels locked, not a long stop. That applies to a person,
an obstacle or a fault.
What makes it worse:
  • The standstill monitor cannot see the slide. Sheet 15 puts both “speed and standstill” sensors on the drive
    wheels. With the brakes locked those wheels read zero while the robot slides. Put at least one channel on a castor
    wheel, which keeps rolling during a slide.
  • Tail first is no fix as drawn. It would make the empty robot lead with its 25 mm rear foam (B-2).
At the map slope of 7.9 %, the same model gives 67 mm from 0.2 m/s at grip 0.30 and 109 mm on wet leaves. So
measuring the real pitch decides most of this, as they say.
Status: a blocker for unattended running, for both designs, until either:
  • the steepest pitch is measured below about 8-9 %; or
  • a braked or weight-shifted fix is designed and tested empty.
Our gate missed this. G4 uses a full bin only, so add an empty-robot stop to the gate for both designs.

B-2. Person protection is still partial (rev 3 B-1, partly fixed)
Geometry from their drawing model (Fig 2):
  • Width. The foam bumper is 700 mm wide on a robot 936 mm wide. A 118 mm strip each side, with the rail ends,
    castors and drive wheels in it, leads with no bumper.
  • Pivot turns. The nose corner is 1.47 m from the axle. At the companion’s pivot rates (0.45 rad/s loaded, 0.6
    empty) it moves at 0.66 / 0.88 m/s, more than the 0.4 m/s rule. It moves sideways, outside the bumper’s plus or
    minus 45 deg active area (Schmersal SSG-SB-L data). Pivot turns happen at the dock, at the bin and at the road
    verge, which is where people are. A nose speed of 0.3 m/s needs a pivot rate of 0.20 rad/s or less, and the sides of
    the foam need to switch.
  • Reversing. Every move onto a bin, and the “tail first above 9 %” rule, leads with 25 mm foam on the two rail
    ends only, plus the open mouth (comb and pad switches, not safety-rated). Their own tail-first stop is 71 mm.
  • Speed rule not safety-rated. The rule lives in ordinary software; they say so in their 4.3. A certified speed
    monitor enforces one fixed limit. Selecting it by slope needs the inclinometer on a safety input.
What to do (any of these):
  • full-width bumper with active side faces;
  • pivot-rate cap in the safety layer;
  • rear foam with at least 100 mm of travel, or a speed cap of about 0.1 m/s for every reverse;
  • or a safety-rated scanner. They price one at EUR 6-7.8k, so the first three are the realistic route.

Major findings
M-1. The lowering protection does not work as drawn
  • The beams are blocked by the bin. In the carry pose of their own model, the bin wheel centre is at x -199.5
    mm and its bottom is 97 mm up. Beam B sits at x -195, under the wheel axle; beam A at -270. Both are 40 mm
    above the ground. As the bin comes down, its own wheel cuts beam B in the last 40 mm and beam A in the
    last 11 mm (Fig 3). Since “lowering is blocked while either beam is broken”, the robot either never sets a 240 L
    bin down, or the beams have to be muted in exactly the last 40 mm, where a foot is crushed. The beams are not in
    any simulation (runY.py, companion: no beam logic).
  • The guard’s window moves with the ground. The guard watches the load only while the bin is more than 15
    mm above “the height at which it left the ground at the pick-up”. The set-down point is a different surface (the
    house end and the road end). If the set-down ground is 20 mm lower, the blind thickness grows from about 20 mm
    to about 40 mm. If it is higher, the guard trips on the bin landing normally.
  • Forces. Even when the guard works, the block carried 107-158 N. That is at or above the BGIA/ISO TS 15066
    draft clamping limits of 125 N (feet), 135 N (hand) and 140 N (lower leg), quoted in NIST IR 8029. A flat hand is
    seen by neither the guard nor the beams; they accept this with a warning sound.
  • Down-current limit. The 3 A limit gives about 516 N of push (LA36: 2.4 A at no load, 10.3 A at 6.8 kN), which
    matches their 0.5 kN. Gravity lowers the bin anyway, so this limit does nothing for a foot under a wheel.
Fix: move the beams clear of the wheel paths, for example one beam just outside each wheel at about 25 mm height,
looking along x. Reference the guard to the comb’s measured height above the local ground, using the leg-tip ToF sensors,
not the pick-up height. Then simulate the beams.

M-2. The speed rule rests on grip, slope and bumper figures the robot cannot know
Grip. Sheet 17 says “no trips with grip below 0.3 until measured”. Nothing on the robot measures grip, and wet leaves
are the norm on a hedged lane from October to December. On 12 % with leaves, the full bin needs 239-277 mm from 0.2
m/s.
Their own runs break the rule. The lane runs used X_VSLOPE ...0.09:0.2:0.15, which is 0.15 m/s empty, nose
first, on the steep pitch. That gives 289 mm against their own 125 mm limit, which they admit.
Bumper figures.
  • The 150 mm after-travel and 0.15 s are ASSUMED. The bumper they cite (Schmersal SSG-SB-L, 150 mm deep)
    has 75-91 mm of after-travel to 250 N, so 150 mm from a 300 mm unit is plausible.
  • That bumper is IP54 and rated 0 to +55 C. That is not an outdoor winter part. Sheet 17 rightly asks for
    “outdoor”, but no such part is named.
  • Its 27-39 mm of switching travel happens before the 0.15 s can start. If the sim starts the clock at first touch, add
    about 20 mm at 0.4 m/s.
  • With 0.20 s reaction the level stop at 0.4 m/s is about 142 mm, against 150 mm.
Force on a person. The spec of 250 N on a 70 mm post matches EN 1525. It exceeds the 140 N lower-leg clamping
figure if the person is pinned against a hedge or gate. Pressure is only about 2.4 N/cm2.

M-3. Bolted corner joints slip, so the fatigue check falls
Each rail-to-cross-member joint has 2 x M8 into the rail and 2 into the member, through top and bottom 6 mm plates, at
25 N m.
  • Preload is about 15.6 kN (K 0.2). Slip resistance per bolt (2 planes, gamma 1.25) is 5.0 kN on painted faces (mu
    0.2) and 7.5 kN at mu 0.3.
  • The sideways tyre force of 904 N at 0.49 m from the mast-base joint gives about 443 N m. With bolts 40-60 mm
    apart that is 7.4-11.1 kN per bolt, so the joint slips into bearing. Bearing is fine: M8 at 14.1 kN per plane.
  • Fatigue. Their check uses detail 90, which needs a slip-resistant preloaded joint. A bearing joint in clearance holes
    is about detail 50: 43.6 MPa against 37 MPa allowed, a ratio of 1.18 (fails). That is roughly the rev 3 welded
    result again.
The load path is our ASSUMPTION.
Fix: blasted, unpainted faying faces (mu at least 0.5 gives 12.5 kN), 3 bolts per member, or fitted bolts. Add locking
nuts and a re-torque schedule.


                                                           5
M-4. The lift stop order is not robust at the top
   • With pins 615 mm apart at q 0, closed 600 puts the actuator’s own stop at q -15. Extended 950 puts it at q +335
     for a full 350 mm stroke, exactly the block. Their +331 assumes a 346 mm stroke.
   • The LA36 data sheet says the stroke “will be up to 4 mm shorter” with end stops. Add up to 2 mm of endplay, pin
     clearance and coast.
   • Monte Carlo (shim_stack): top margin to the block is -2.0 / +0.5 / +3.0 mm (1st/50th/99th percentile). The
     chance that the actuator’s own stop lands on the block is about 38 % (Fig 4).
   • The bottom is fine (+0.4 to +2.6 mm). The switch-before-actuator margin is +1.8 to +6.2 mm.
The consequence is mild. The switches at 327 cut the feed first, and the 6 A up-limit caps the push at about 3.1 kN. But
the claimed order “actuator off before the blocks” is not guaranteed.
Fix: raise the top blocks to +340 and the switch bodies to at least +345. The top tie is at 386, so there is room. That
gives a top margin of +3.0 to +8.0 mm.

M-5. The time window is misread, and it is our companion’s fault
The project’s bye-law: bins out no earlier than 18:00 the day before, back by 21:00 on collection day.
   • Drops have no end time before the crew comes. Three drops at 72 min from 18:00 end at 21:36, which is legal.
   • Returns can start as soon as the crew has passed. Three returns at 72 min must start by 17:24.
The “two bins fit, third does not” result comes from b2f_companion.op_preflight. That code refuses any trip starting
06:00-18:00, or ending after 21:00 (assuming 50 min). It is B2-F code that both of us inherited (Fig 5).
So decision 1, “castor brakes to get the third bin back”, loses its time argument. Castor brakes may still be needed for
B-1.
Two things are needed in the companion: the real trip time in place of c + 50, and the bye-law as two windows (drop:
18:00 onward; return: crew passed, until 21:00). Brian should confirm the crew’s usual time.

M-6. Cost understated

Watch dealer listing starts at EUR 771 ex VAT. Our G6 BOM had EUR 1,058. Their EUR 400-700 is low.

M-7. Only very low edges can be climbed from a standstill
Their momentum explanation is right: with a full bin, 40 mm needs 95 N m at 878 N per wheel (125 N m on their 1,160
N), against 60 N m. Quasi-statically, though, the limits are lower:
  • Full bin: about 15 mm (60 N m).
  • Empty: about 12 mm in the dry (castor push equals 497 N of grip) and about 4 mm in the wet.
Their “20 mm empty, 25 mm full, square on” passes therefore also depend on speed. A robot stopped in front of such an
edge, for example after a person stop, may not restart nose first. Tyre compliance helps, and these figures are conservative.
Fix: make “every edge over about 10 mm is ramped” a lane condition, or test restart-from-standstill at 20 and 25 mm,
for both designs.


Minor findings
  • m-1. Nose cross-member. It is still 50x50x4 on the drawings, at SF 1.55. A 60x60x4 member gives SF 2.43 by
    our section moduli (8,854 to 13,884 mm3) and adds 1.0 kg, not 0.8. The load case already includes 3x the static
    castor load, so this is their own rule, not a failure.
  • m-2. Mass rows. The LA36 weighs 5.75 kg for 350 mm (4.55 kg plus 0.24 kg per 50 mm, as listed by eBoat
    Watch from LINAK data), so -0.75 kg. Crush tubes at 0.02 kg are light: a 20x2x60 mm steel tube is about 0.054 kg,
    so +1.3 kg for 40.
  • m-3. Top tie. It is fixed by one M8 into a tapped hole in each aluminium stop block, which also takes the 4.8 kN
    stop load. Use a steel insert or a through-bolt. No bolt locking or re-torque interval is given anywhere on sheet 17.
  • m-4. Lift current limits (sheet 15: 6 A up, 3 A down). The LA36 draws 2.4 A at no load and up to 3x more in
    the cold (data sheet). Lowering at 3 A may stall at 0-5 C. Set both limits by a bench test at 0 C.
  • m-5. Bumper height. It spans 115-265 mm. Toes and low kerbs pass under it; the castor is about 400 mm behind
    its face, which is enough by our numbers. Kerbs over 115 mm at the road, and brambles, will cause nuisance trips.
  • m-6. Length 1,831 mm.
       – Pivot circle 2.94 m (theirs: 3.00), so 0.33 m to each hedge in a 3.6 m lane. The nose radius is 0.27 m more
          than rev 3.
       – Pivot spots must stay at least 1.5 m back from the [road] edge.
       – The companion’s 0.15 m sweep-clearance check should use the new outline.
  • m-7. Castor brakes. The idea is sound for B-1 (all-wheel braking: 41 mm from 0.2 m/s on 12 % wet), but:
       – an electric brake on a free-swivel castor needs a slip ring or a limited swivel;
       – it must be spring-applied;
       – braking a leading castor when reversing can flip it.
       – No catalogue part exists, as they say. Moving 10.6 kg of battery and electronics aft (hold to 14 % wet) is
          cheap, but it is not enough alone.
  • m-8. PL estimate. Fine as a first pass. It leaves out the slope selection, the brake test (their 4.4) and the slide
    blindness (B-1). ISO 3691-4 was not applied, as they say.
  • m-9. Clash results. Only 0.53 cm3 of comb and nib into the bin rim (a load contact in the sim pose) and 0.19
    cm3 of sensor post into the LTE antenna. Both are trivial. Docking geometry with the nose pads is not drawn or
    checked here.


What revision 4 got right (confirmed)
  1. Weight: 177.61 kg from their table. The fabricated rows re-derive from solids and density, and the RHS rows
     match catalogue kg/m (mass_check_r4.json).
  2. Lift sweep (0.5 mm steps): bottom switches at -25, blocks at -18.5, blocks at +335.5, switch bodies at +339, top
     tie at 386. Nothing else touches.
  3. Castor swivel: 360 deg is clear of the new bumper carrier, the charge pads and the floor cut-outs.
  4. Static clash: only legacy pairs, plus two trivial contacts.
  5. Stopping distances: our hand model reproduces their 159-stop map within 3-10 % on the cases checked.
  6. B-1 is real and shared with B2-F. Finding it was good work.
  7. The 40 mm step claim is true: it was only crossed with momentum.
  8. Withdrawals of 80 N m and the boost are right (70 A against the 60 A BMS).
  9. LA36 data: 6.8 kN, self-lock 8.8 kN, IP66, 600 mm built-in, 12.7 / 15.7 mm/s.
 10. Pad springs: 15.8 mm before solid; 768 MPa at full stroke against 925 MPa.

 11. Structure rows reproduced:
       • motor plate 150 MPa, SF 2.37;
       • strut Euler load 205 kN against 6.4 kN;
       • stop-block bolts 4.0 kN prying plus 2.4 kN shear, against 33 kN;
       • rail SF 2.10.
 12. Bolt torques match 8.8 lightly-oiled tables: M8 25, M10 49, M12 85 N m; M4 12.9 at 4.5 N m.
 13. Safety chain: 10 monitored inputs, the lift feed through safety relays, and diverse tilt sensing.
 14. Self-corrections are clear: castor load averaging, the round-tyre claim, the comb sentence.


For the other agent (short fix list)
  1. Measure first. Have Brian measure the steepest pitch and the highest edge. Add an empty-robot stop and hold
     test to the gate, for both designs.
  2. Standstill monitoring. Put a speed/standstill channel on a castor wheel so a slide is seen.
  3. Bumper coverage. Make it full width with active sides. Cap the pivot rate at 0.20 rad/s or less in the safety
     layer. Give the rear foam at least 100 mm of travel, or cap reversing at about 0.1 m/s. Do not use tail-first until
     the rear is protected.
  4. Beams and guard. Move the beams off the bin-wheel line and simulate them. Reference the lowering guard to
     the local ground, not the pick-up height.
  5. Corner joints. Make them slip-resistant (blasted faces or 3 bolts) and redo the fatigue check. Add locking and
     re-torque.
  6. Lift stops. Raise the top blocks to +340 and the switch bodies to at least +345.
  7. Companion. Use the real trip time in place of c + 50, and code the bye-law as two windows. Redo the “third bin”
     conclusion.
  8. Costs and drawings. Re-price the LA36 (about EUR 940-1,060). Draw the 60x60 nose member. Name an
     outdoor-rated bumper (below 0 C, IP65 or better).
