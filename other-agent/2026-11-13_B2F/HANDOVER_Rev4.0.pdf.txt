1. Read this first
   • Current design: B2-F. It is a wheeled carrier. It docks on the bin’s front wall, lifts the bin by the EN 840
     comb receiver, and carries it clear of the ground.
   • B2-F is provisional. It is not proven until test B0: Brian checks which wall of his own bins carries the
     lifting lip. This takes about 5 minutes.
   • Nothing has been built or field-tested. Every result here comes from simulation: MuJoCo physics,
     ArduPilot SITL and Python models.
   • The lane has not been surveyed. Its route comes from OpenStreetMap and its heights from EU-DEM.
     Width, hedges, steps, trench, pothole and wet-leaf patches are all assumed.
   • Cost today: EUR 3,844 with a donor powerchair drive, or EUR 4,112 with a new drive. With the pending
     structural upgrades (S12) it is about EUR 3,976 / 4,245. The gap to the EUR 2,000 target is EUR 1,844 to
     2,245.
   • Status: concept, not for manufacture.

2. The job
Brian lives in [village], [county]. BinRobo moves his 140 L and 240 L wheelie bins ([waste collector]) along a private
hedged lane to the [road] road. The lane is about 633 m long and about 3.6 m wide.
     • The trips: it drops the full bins and later brings the empties back, often in the dark.
     • Bye-laws: bins go out no earlier than 18:00 the day before collection and come back by 21:00 on collection
       day.
     • Conditions: 12 % slopes, wet leaves, 40 mm steps, and people, cars and dogs on the lane.
     • Navigation and monitoring: RTK GNSS, lidar and camera; 4G monitoring and recordings.
Brian’s standing rules:
     • Design for the end product. No mule or prototype stage (2026-11-10).
     • No capability or safety cuts to save money. Cost-downs come only from sourcing, used parts and materials.
     • Wheels only. Cheapest workable, not over-engineered.
     • Private use (confirmed 2026-11-11). Target under EUR 2,000, reported honestly.
     • “Do what you think is best and test it thoroughly.”

4. Current design: B2-F
4.1 Concept
B2 turned round so it docks on the bin’s front wall, opposite the handle, hinge and wheels. A comb plate engages
the EN 840 receiver lip and a pad clamps the bin. A 250 mm-stroke linear actuator then lifts the bin so its wheels
clear the ground, and the robot drives the bin back to the road.

4.3 Drive, power, safety
  • Drive: a donor powerchair, or a new PEWM82M-320W pair (US$298) with a 3.5:1 chain (about EUR 130).
    Needs about 24.6 N m per wheel against a 31 N m spec. On a 12 % climb at 110 kg the motors draw 12.3 A.
  • Battery: 24 V LFP, 512 Wh (8 x EVE C40). It cannot charge below 0 deg C, so the dock needs a heater.
  • Lift actuator: 4 kN unit in the BOM. The sim caps force at 3 kN. Peak load seen is 2.2 kN in normal carry,
    with contact spikes of 2.4-3.0 kN.
  • Safety (G8 lean):
      – Pilz PNOZ X3 relay (Cat 4 / PL e device, used, 3-month warranty).
      – E-stop plus four Omron bumper switches.
      – Two series contactors and spring brakes.
      – Three-layer speed limit: about 1 m/s from the gearing, 0.5 m/s in the controller, and the monitor trips
         above 0.6 m/s.
      – The speed monitor and contactor checking are uncertified (estimated PL c-d).
  • Navigation: ArduPilot Rover 4.6.2, RTK GNSS, lidar, camera, a companion computer, and SIM7600G-H 4G.

4.4 Known open design items
  • S12 structure (target SF >= 2): these upgrades are needed but are not yet in the CAD or sims:
      – rails 50x50x4;
      – mast uprights 60x60x4 plus 80x40x4;
      – through-plates or crush tubes at the mast-foot bolts;
      – comb plate 8 mm;
      – pivot pin D20;
      – 10 mm backing flats at the rail screws.
    Together they add about EUR 115 and 19 kg.
  • Nose length: 1,106 mm including bumpers, which is 16 mm over the 1.09 m spec.
  • Drawings: the B2 handle keeper is drawn but inactive, and the comb buffer is not drawn.
  • Drive-axle suspension or load: none fitted today. The lane physics asks for it (section 5.2).

5.2 Lane physics, MuJoCo digital twin (13 Nov): 11 of 18 pass
   • Pass:
        – P1, a 240 L bin at 110 kg, home to pad A.
        – P3, a 140 L bin at 66 kg.
        – P4a/b, stop and hold on a 12 % slope on wet leaves (0.5 mm creep).
        – P5a/b, sharp 40 mm drop, but with only about 3 mm of bin-wheel clearance.
        – P5c/f, 1:4 ramps.
        – P5h, the 60 mm pothole with a full bin.
        – P6a, a person steps out: e-stop in 0.116 m.
        – P6b, a dog crosses.
   • Fail:
        – P2a and P2c, the empty return and the empty-bin pickup: the pothole traps the light robot.
        – P5d/e, the sharp 40 mm up-step: it needs grip of 0.86 and the ground gives 0.6. A 4 kN actuator does
           not help.
        – P5g/i, the open trench: a drive wheel wedges across the 200 mm gap.
        – P7, night with worst-case load and 20 % less grip: trapped in the pothole.
   • Extra runs with the pothole filled: both fail. The rigid four-point chassis rocks, so a drive wheel unloads
     on wet leaves and the robot slips off line.
   • Root cause of every failure: the drive base on rough or slippery ground. None comes from the grab, lift or
     receiver.
   • Fixes recommended:
        – Ramp or fill every edge over 25 mm on the lane.
        – Add drive-axle suspension or load.
        – Add slip and turn-rate limiting to the drive control.
        – Keep wet leaves off the slope.
     Then re-run P2, P5 and P7.

5.3 ArduPilot Rover SITL (13 Nov): 52 of 60 pass
These are 20 scenarios, each on seeds 7, 11 and 23. The code was md5-frozen before scoring.
  • All safety and refusal scenarios passed 3/3:
      – RTK float and long loss;
      – person, dog and car;
      – companion-computer freeze;
      – e-stop;
      – low battery;
      – missing, tipped or blocked bin;
      – wind and frost no-go;
      – geofence.
  • Fail:
      – s3a, three-bin drop, 0/3. The 140 L home spot is 4.6 m off the lane centre, and lateral moves are
          rate-limited. The robot came within 0.012-0.071 m of the hedge (limit 0.124 m), so the fence held it.
      – s1b and s3b, pickups, 1/3 each. The lidar fixes the bin’s position once, at an angle, from 4.5-8 m.
          That leaves 0.03-0.12 m of error, so the robot correctly refused to grab.
      – s4b, RTK loss, seed 23. A sign error steers the robot toward the right hedge.
  • Good:
      – Hedge clearance 0.196-0.413 m elsewhere; pad A 0.38-0.41 m after a fix.
      – Set-downs within 0.006-0.054 m.
      – 44.7-46.7 Wh per trip.
      – The three-bin pickup finished at 20:47, inside the 21:00 bye-law.
  • Fixes needed: all software.
      – Start the lateral move earlier from off-centre home spots.
      – Re-measure the bin close up before the grab.
      – Fix the lateral sign error.

10. Main risks
  • Receiver wall: if B0 shows the wrong wall, the design changes.
  • The lane itself: sharp edges, the trench, potholes and wet leaves. Today the drive base fails on these without
    lane preparation.
  • Actuator: no cold-force figure at -20 deg C.
  • Donor parts: unmeasured donor motors, and a used safety relay.
  • Cost: about double the target, and many prices are unverified search excerpts.
  • Safety: the case is hand-calculated (PL d target), not SISTEMA-verified.
  • Sim gap: simulation is not the real world; field testing is still needed.
