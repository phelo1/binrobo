# Research notes, 12 November 2026

Condensed from a web research task run on 12 November 2026 for the Rev H against B2 comparison. Prices are asking prices or search snippets seen that day, in the original currency. Nothing here is legal advice. Sources are listed at the end; go to them before relying on any figure.

The research task proposed a design it called "Rev I" at EUR 1,900 to 2,200. That estimate uses a thinner safety and wiring list than the project's G8 baseline, so it is **not** comparable with the Rev H2 and B2 totals. Its useful ideas are folded into Rev H2 or listed as gated savings.

## Q1. Which side carries the comb receiver?

The front wall, opposite the handle, lid hinge and wheels. See `EVIDENCE_receiver_side.md`. Caveat: the research tool could not read drawings, so its conclusion rests on text in EN 840-1 (terminology), the GGAWB guidelines and the ESE manual. The drawings in the project's own data sheets agree.

## Q2. What is the receiver rated for?

- There is no rating in kN. The container must carry 0.4 kg/dm³ x nominal volume (EN 840-1 clause 6): 96 kg of fill in a 240 L bin, 110 kg gross. GGAWB permissible totals: 120 L 60 kg, 140 L 70 kg, 240 L 110 kg.
- EN 840-5 (2004 text read in full; 2020 snippets show the same clauses): at least 100 loaded lift-tilt cycles on a standard comb lifter, at least 5 empty; plastic receiver deflection no more than 1.5 % of its length; tests at 23 °C and -18 °C; 1,000 kerb drops of 140 mm; each wheel 100 kg static.
- GGAWB s.3.5: the whole receiver must be supported across its width by comb teeth; receiver spread angle limited to 25 deg.
- The bin must lock into the lifter during lifting (EN 840-1 clause 5.2). ESE gives comb-to-locking-bar gaps of 23 mm and 8 mm, useful if a keeper is added.
- The handle of a 2-wheel bin is tested for pulling only.

## Q5. Kerb climbing

- Published powerchair ratings: 300 mm drive wheels 70 to 75 mm (Quickie Q300 M Mini); 355 mm wheels 100 mm; small-wheel chairs 25 to 50 mm.
- The ratings assume a square approach with a run-up, castors touching first, then power applied steadily.
- Rigid-wheel statics: a 165 mm radius wheel carrying 700 N needs about 75 N m to pull itself over a 40 mm edge from rest, more than the project's 60 N m cap. That agrees with the MuJoCo stall, and it ignores tyre envelopment and momentum.
- A pushed 260 mm castor needs a horizontal force of about 1.04 x its load to mount 40 mm; a 200 mm castor about 1.33 x.
- Cheap aids: pneumatic block-tread tyres, a short run-up, a kerb-climber skid, a ramp at the worst step. The research also suggested approaching drive-wheels-first; the Rev H2 reduced-order model does **not** support that (tail-first fails at 50 mm where nose-first passes), so it is left to simulation S6 and test F1.

## Q6. Regulation

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027 and replaces Directive 2006/42/EC. Its definition of manufacturer includes someone who builds machinery and puts it into service for their own use. The own-use exemption in Article 18 covers modifying a machine, not building one. Enforcement against a private one-off on a private lane is unlikely; liability after an incident is the real exposure. Keep a written risk assessment.
- Rated needed for a private one-off: an E-stop that removes drive power through a contactor; spring-applied brakes; contact bumpers in the stop chain; a firmware speed cap.
- Rated recommended: a used dual-channel safety relay.
- Rated optional: a second series contactor; an independent speed monitor. **Rev H2 keeps both** (about EUR 69 of parts), because the project's own safety case (`ELEC_SAFETY_G7.md`) calls for them and dropping them is the owner's decision.
