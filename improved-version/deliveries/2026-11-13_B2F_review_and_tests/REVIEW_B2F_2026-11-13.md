# Review of BinRobo B2-F (Handover Rev 4.0)

13 November 2026. Reviewer: the Rev H / Rev H2 author. Reviewed: `HANDOVER_Rev4.0.pdf`, `BinRobo_B2F_drawings_A3.pdf`, `BinRobo_B2F_BOM_2026-11-12.xlsx`, the lane-physics and SITL result reports, and the renders in `BinRobo_B2F_package_2026-11-13.zip`.

## 1. Verdict

- **B2-F should be the one design going forward.** It docks on the correct wall, and in the project's own MuJoCo gate it ties Rev H2 at 8 of 9 and beats it on lane fit (12/12 against 9/12) and on perturbations (24/24 against 23/24). I am not proposing Rev H2 as a rival any more; what is left of it is offered below as simplifications to B2-F.
- **Nothing in this review was simulated.** The package holds reports, drawings, STEP files and the parts list, not the source code, and MuJoCo is not installed in my session. Every design change below is a proposal to be run through the frozen gate. The cost arithmetic is reproduced exactly from the workbook (EUR 3,844.23 and EUR 4,112.32).
- **About EUR 390 to 460 can come off without removing a function or a safety layer**: to about EUR 3,460 (donor drive) or EUR 3,650 (new drive, AGM battery). EUR 225 of that is pricing and dead parts and needs no simulation; the largest item in it (EUR 120 on the frame) needs a local quote to confirm. The pending S12 structural upgrades add about EUR 130 back.
- **EUR 2,000 is still out of reach under Brian's rule of no capability or safety cuts.** Safety, navigation, sensing, comms, wiring, lights and dock alone are about EUR 1,630 of parts before a motor, a frame or a lift is bought.
- **The two most valuable next steps cost nothing and are Brian's**: test B0 (which wall carries the lip) and the lane survey. Seven of the 18 lane-physics runs fail, all of them at a step, trench, pothole or wet-leaf patch whose size and position are assumed, not measured.

## 2. Design findings, most important first

| # | Finding | Evidence in the package | Proposed change | Cost | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The lift stroke is too short.** The bin is lifted 71 mm, not the intended 100 mm, with the actuator at 239 of 250 mm. The comb tops out at 1,105 mm; an EN 840 lip can sit at 1,030 mm, leaving 75 mm. This is why bin-wheel clearance falls to 2.9 mm on a 40 mm drop | Handover 4.2; lane physics F5, P5a | Use the full 400 mm stroke of the unit the parts list already prices (line B-0 is "priced as the 400 mm unit"). Comb range about 855 to 1,205 mm, carry lift 100 to 120 mm. Estimated clearance on the 40 mm drop: 30 to 50 mm | EUR 0 | Gate G1, G6, G8; lane P5a. The combined centre of gravity rises about 20 mm, which should cost under 1 deg of tip margin |
| 2 | **Rigid four-wheel chassis.** On twisted ground one drive wheel unloads to 0 N and the robot yaws off the lane when empty | Lane physics F4 | A rocker on the castor pair (the report itself lists it as an option): castor cross-beam on a centre pivot, bump stops at about +/-25 mm. All four wheels then keep their share of load on any twist. Prefer this to drive-axle suspension, which costs more and lets the comb height wander | About EUR 20 | Lane P2, P7; gate G8 (tip angle with the stops) |
| 3 | **The pad does not need an actuator.** The toggle-locked retracting pad was needed on the handle side, where the bin's wheels had to pass it. On the front wall nothing passes it | Rev H2 scored G1 89/90 and G6 18/18 in the same gate with a fixed pad | Fixed, depth-adjustable pad (or the sprung pad with a travel stop). Removes one actuator, the toggle, the lock switch and firmware step F10 | Saves EUR 80 | Gate G1, G6 on B2-F with the pad fixed |
| 4 | **Profile rails on 2 mm tube.** S12 already asks for 10 mm backing flats under the HGR15 screws | Drawing notes; S12 list | Roller carriage on the mast uprights | Saves EUR 65 and the backing flats | Bench test for binding under load. Gate unaffected |
| 5 | **Dead parts from the towing design** are still drawn and priced: the handle keeper ("inactive in B2-F"), the cam gearmotor for the pawl lift, the second hook-latch switch | Drawing notes; lines GR-07, K2 | Delete from CAD and parts list. Check line G13 (seat and wheel-contact switches) against B1-SW and B-4 for a duplicate | Saves EUR 19 | Nothing |
| 6 | **Empty, only half the weight is on the drive wheels** (455 N of about 905 N), and the 5 kg ballast sits at the nose | Lane physics F4 | With the rocker fitted, re-check whether the ballast is still needed once S12 adds 19 kg. In the donor variant, try the chair's 21 to 30 kg AGM pair beside the drive wheels, not in the nose | EUR 0 | Gate G2, G8; lane P2 |
| 7 | **Kerb, trench and pothole results come from rigid wheels on assumed obstacles.** Powerchairs with 300 mm drive wheels are rated for 70 mm kerbs | Lane physics F1 to F3 and section 4 | Survey the lane before designing for these. Then ramps, covers and fill where needed (EUR 20 to 40 each, not in the parts list), pneumatic block-tread tyres on the new-drive variant, and the slip and yaw-rate limiting already recommended | Lane prep not priced | Survey; a real 40 mm kerb test |
| 8 | **Documents disagree on the actuator**: 250 mm stroke in the hand-over and CAD, "300 mm, priced as 400 mm" in the parts list, 3 kN cap in the simulation, 4 kN unit bought | Handover 4.2, 4.3; line B-0 | Settle on one unit and one stroke (finding 1) and re-run with its real force limit | - | - |

Not design faults, but they should be said plainly to Brian: the chain stage that caps the donor's speed is carried at EUR 90, which looks low for two stub axles, four bearings, four sprockets, chains and guards; the donor chair at EUR 400 is unverified; 72 % of the parts value is still unverified, which is why contingency is EUR 380.

| FAB-B2 frame | 354.12 | 234.12 | The line is copied from the Rev G8 frame, which included a EUR 120 press-brake set-up. B2-F is described as tube plus flat laser-cut plates. **Confirm there are no folded parts and get a local quote** |

Simplifications that need a gate or bench run (EUR 145 of parts): pad actuator, toggle and lock switch (EUR 80); profile rails to rollers (EUR 65).

Considered and not recommended, because each removes something: a cheaper motor controller without encoder inputs or current telemetry (EUR 72); dropping the second contactor or the speed monitor; dropping the chain stage, which is the mechanical layer of the speed limit.

With 1 and 2 the first runs would be, in order: reproduce one stored B2-F gate result; findings 1, 2 and 3 through G1, G6 and G8; lane P2, P5a and P7; then the cost figure again.
