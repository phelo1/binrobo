# Verdict

**Revision 2 scores 48 / 60 on the frozen SITL criteria (ArduPilot Rover 4.6.2, frozen scorer md5 2c4d86d4).**
All 48 fault and hazard runs pass. All 12 full missions (s1a, s1b, s3a, s3b x seeds 7, 11, 23) complete their plan,
dock, place every bin inside the limits and are home before 21:00, and every one of them fails on one criterion only:
**C6, energy per bin trip, 50.73 to 52.86 Wh against the frozen 50 Wh limit.** The cause is mass (0.21 Wh per kg of robot
per trip); the battery itself is not short (a three-bin evening ends at 55 to 56 % SOC).

Three points qualify the result:

1. **The pick-up line-up now leans on a rear camera model that is optimistic.** The lidar-and-EKF line-up alone was more than
   50 mm off (the C8 limit) in 9 of 27 scored approaches. Every one was rescued by the new rear-camera correction (RCB), which is
   modelled as truth plus 10 mm Gaussian noise, with no bias, no misdetection and no yaw error, and whose random-number generator
   re-seeds at every chunk resume, so in our chunked runs it re-used the same one or two draws. With the generator fixed and
   15 mm noise (3 runs), one bad correction pushed the robot 59 mm off; the frozen pre-grab gate caught it and the retry passed.
2. **The energy guard under-budgets.** It still admits a trip on a fixed 50 Wh budget; real trips need up to 52.9 Wh. About
   58 Wh is the budget needed (worst trip plus 10 %), or about 106 kg robot mass to get trips under 50 Wh.
3. **Stress results are safe but thin** (2 to 4 runs each): low light refuses at the grab gate; camera noise x1.5 and lidar noise
   x1.5 with 10 % dropout fail C6 only; at 122.4 kg 1 of 4 runs passes (s4b) and the three missions fail C6 only.

Recommendation: accept the driving-software behaviour as safe; do not count C6 as passed; raise the energy-guard budget to
about 58 Wh (or take ~13 kg out) and agree a new trip limit; before relying on the camera, replace the camera model with a
measured one (bias, misdetection, yaw) and fix the RNG.

# 3. Vision and lidar

## 3.1 What the code does

Revision 2 adds a rear docking camera check (switch X_FIX_RCB) at the pre-grab pose 1.2 m out on the bin axis. It measures the
lateral offset of the bin axis; if the reading is more than 25 mm (CAM_TOL), it shifts the bin estimate by the measured
difference and re-aligns (forward 0.8 m, reverse back), up to two checks; then the frozen truth gate (C8: 50 mm, 3 deg) runs,
with its own one retry. It also widens the approach acceptance (APP: along-track lateral fraction 0.15 -> 0.40), so the robot
accepts a looser approach and relies on the camera to tidy it.

## 3.2 Lidar-only line-up

Before any camera correction, the lidar-refined bin estimate plus EKF left the robot more than 50 mm off the true pre-grab axis in
**9 of 27 scored approaches** (worst 83 mm, s3a seed 11; mean 39 mm), and in 4 of 16 approaches in the stress/variant runs.
Without the camera those 9 would have hit the frozen gate outside its envelope at the first attempt. The camera is therefore
load-bearing for C8, not a refinement.

## 3.3 Camera noise model: optimistic

The simulated camera reading is lat_true + N(0, 10 mm). It has:

- no bias (lens / mounting / bin-shape offset), no yaw error, no outliers, no misdetection, no lighting or rain dependence;
- full trust: the whole measured difference is applied as the correction, with no plausibility check against the lidar.

In the scored runs the camera error was at most 10 mm (mean 4.6 mm over 46 checks). That is the model, not evidence about a
real camera. A real rear camera reading a bin edge at 1.2 m in the dark is unlikely to be this clean.

## 3.4 RNG re-seed at chunk resume

The camera noise generator (_cam_rng) is an attribute of the companion object, created on first use from the run seed. Our runs
are chunked (one SITL chunk per foreground call; resume from checkpoint), and every resume builds a new companion, so the generator
re-seeds and the camera re-uses the first one or two draws of that seed at every grab (seed 7: -0.3 mm then +6.0 mm). Only 10
distinct camera errors occur in 46 scored checks. Their own runs were unchunked (budget 100000 s) and are not affected; our
scored verdicts are, in the sense that the camera noise they saw was not independent between grabs.

Correction (our copy b2f_companion_xs.py only, switch X_STRESS_CAMRNG_PERSIST=1): a fresh, checkpoint-safe draw per check,
seeded by (seed, 7919, number of checks so far), with sigma 15 mm. Three runs (s1a s11, s1b s23, s3b s7) all completed and
failed C6 only:

# 5. Energy-guard analysis

- **Trip energy.** 24 mission trips: 50.73 to 52.86 Wh, mean 51.79. Replaying the frozen energy model on the traces
  (scripts_rev2/rev2_energy_split.py): a trip is about 27 Wh traction (24.5 to 24.9 Wh of it due to the robot's own mass) and about
  25 Wh time-based load over 55 to 58 minutes. Robot-mass share: **0.205 to 0.209 Wh per kg per trip**. The longer lift and
  set-down times (x1.4) add time-based load as well.
- **Mass needed for 50 Wh.** Worst trip 52.86 Wh: 2.86 / 0.207 = 13.8 kg out, i.e. about **106 kg**. The +3 kg audit run
  (122.4 kg) added 0.6 to 0.7 Wh per trip, consistent with the slope.
- **The guard.** The companion admits a trip if available energy (above reserve) is at least a fixed 50 Wh budget. s8 passes
  (17 % SOC, 32.4 Wh available < 50, refused). From the guard log the pack is about 465 Wh with about 47 Wh reserve. A trip
  admitted at 50 to 53 Wh available would therefore dip up to 3 Wh into the reserve: not a stranding risk, but it breaks the
  intent of C6's third clause. **Budget needed: about 58 Wh** (worst trip x 1.1). Raising the guard budget does not change any
  verdict here; it only makes the guard honest. C6's 50 Wh trip limit itself needs a decision by the owner of the criteria.

# 8. Caveats

- **Firmware.** Ours: ArduRover 4.6.2, the frozen binary. Theirs: 4.7.2. Their stored 54 runs re-score to the same verdicts,
  but individual values differ (e.g. s3a seed 7 hedge 0.213 m theirs, 0.152 m ours). Chunked resumes (ours) also restart SITL and
  the EKF from a checkpoint pose; that is a second difference from their unchunked runs.
- **Kinematic SITL.** The world is kinematic: no tyre slip, no castor scrub, no bin dynamics, no kerb or pothole. Energy is a
  frozen model replayed on the motion, not a measured pack. Grabbing and lifting are scored by the C8 envelope handed to the
  MuJoCo gate, not simulated here.
- **Sensor models.** Camera and lidar are simple Gaussian models (3.3); stress runs are 2 to 4 runs each, seed 7 mostly.
- **Truncated trace.** s3a seed 7 result.json is valid (scored PASS of all but C6) but its trace.json was cut mid-write by a
  timeout, so it has no video; seeds 11 and 23 are shown instead.
- **Files.** Runs: /workspace/work/out/rev2/sitl/runs (60), `runs_{m122,cam15,lid15,night,camfix}`; scores `score.json`/`score.md` and
  `var_<profile>/score.json`; `metrics_<profile>.json` (scripts_rev2/rev2_metrics.py).
- **Log.** docs/reviews/PROGRESS_rev2_sitl.md.
  Render scripts: sitl/b2f/render/rev2_render_one.sh and rev2_render_part.sh (wrappers of b2f_video_rev2.py; variant runs
  rendered through symlinks in /workspace/work/out/rev2/sitl/_vr_render/).
